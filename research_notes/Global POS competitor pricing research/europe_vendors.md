Where the summary said the figure sits on the vendor's own page I mark it **[vendor page, via summary]**; otherwise **[third party, via summary]**. > - The session's web-search budget ran out before the last three searches (fiscal compliance, Flatpay funding detail, Qlub/Tevalis/Trivec). Those topics are listed under Gaps, not filled from memory.

## Key question 1 — Which vendors were covered, and what is each one (markets, target customer)?

### Takeaway
Of the ~35 named vendors, usable price data was found for about 22; six have no public price (quote-only) and five returned nothing at all. The European market splits into (a) payment-funded "free POS" players (SumUp, Zettle/PayPal, Square, Revolut, myPOS, Flatpay, Teya, Dojo), (b) subscription POS at roughly €35–250/month (Lightspeed, Epos Now, orderbird, ready2order, Tabology, TouchBistro, French restaurant tills), and (c) freemium/cheap software (Loyverse, Hiboutik, Odoo).

## Key question 2 — Prices per vendor: software per register/location, free tiers, hardware, card rates, contracts, add-ons

### Takeaway
"Free software + 1.3–1.75% card fee" is the entry norm; paid POS software in Europe clusters at about €35–90/month ($39–99) per till for small-business products and €79–250 ($87–275) for Lightspeed-class systems. lebanon-POS at $15–35 per till sits below every paid European plan found except Hiboutik (€9.90–12.90), Loyverse add-ons ($5 each), Odoo (€19.90/user) and Dojo's promotional EPOS (£11.99).

### Inferences
- Benchmark for lebanon-POS ($15–35/till): cheaper than orderbird PRO (~$39), ready2order (~$39–52 before the mandatory German TSE add-on), Vectron's all-in rental (~$33–44 incl. hardware and service), Tabology (~$51+), SumUp POS Pro (~$64), Lightspeed (~$87+), Square Restaurants Plus (~$90). It is more expensive than the free tiers of SumUp, Zettle, Square, Revolut, Loyverse, Hiboutik and Odoo, all of which are (except Loyverse, Hiboutik, Odoo) subsidised by card fees that a cash-heavy Lebanese merchant would not generate.
- Vectron's €29.90–39.90 bundle including the till hardware shows that incumbents can match low software prices when payments revenue is bundled; a software-only entrant cannot rely on price alone in card-heavy markets.

Not found for SumUp, Lightspeed (a UK loyalty pricing page exists but its figures were not in the summaries), Epos Now, Zettle, Shopify.

- Square pricing for FR, ES, IE: not found (only UK).

## Key question 4 — Business models: payments vs subscriptions, free POS, price changes 2023–2026

### Takeaway
The dominant European small-merchant model is free or near-free POS software paid for by a 1.3–1.75% card fee, with an optional monthly fee that buys a lower rate (SumUp £19 → 0.99%, myPOS €19/39 → 0.89%/0.79%, Dojo £39.99 flat). Pure-subscription vendors (Lightspeed, Epos Now, ready2order, orderbird) charge €35–250 and increasingly push their own payments, sometimes penalising customers who do not use them.

### Cited Findings
- Payment-funded free POS: SumUp POS Lite (free software), Zettle (no monthly fee), Square free plan, Revolut POS (free with business account), myPOS One (€0), Dojo Essential EPOS (free), Flatpay (no monthly subscription per trusted.de) — [SumUp POS Lite](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/payment-solutions-made-simple/pos-lite/); [PayPal UK POS pricing](https://www.paypal.com/gb/business/pos-system/pricing); [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/payment-processing/best-pos-systems-restaurants/); [Revolut in-person](https://revolut.com/business/in-person/); [myPOS AT fees](https://mypos.com/en-at/pricing-and-fees/fees); [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/uk/merchant-accounts/dojo-review); [trusted.de](https://trusted.de/flatpay)
- "Pay a monthly fee, get a lower rate": SumUp Payments Plus £19/month → 0.99%; myPOS Plus €19 → 0.89% and Pro €39 → 0.79% — [SumUp vs Zettle](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/compare/sumup-vs-zettle/); [myPOS AT fees](https://mypos.com/en-at/pricing-and-fees/fees)
- Banking as lock-in: SumUp cuts the rate from 1.69% to 1.49% if payouts go to its own Business Account; merchant deposits in SumUp business accounts passed €1 billion (German press release, 2025); SumUp launched cash deposits for merchants in the UK, Italy, Spain and France (Dec 2025) — [SumUp pricing](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/pricing/?prc=UK19); [SumUp press](https://www.sumup.com/de-de/press/business-account-growth-2025/)
- Minimum-volume fees on "free" plans: Teya charges £29.99/month if monthly card turnover is under £2,500; Flatpay reportedly charges extra below €2,000 turnover — [SumUp vs Teya](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/compare/sumup-vs-teya/); [trusted.de](https://trusted.de/flatpay)
- Subscription vendors penalising non-use of in-house payments: an Epos Now customer on a prepaid annual licence reports a monthly fee added mid-contract because they did not use Epos Now card payments (single Trustpilot review) — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/eposnow.com?page=7)
- Charging the diner instead of the merchant: sunday applies a guest fee so it can offer restaurants lower card fees — [sunday](https://sundayapp.com/en-gb/pay-at-table/)
- Scale and funding: SumUp about $600M revenue in 2025 (Sacra estimate), EBITDA-positive since Dec 2022, last widely reported valuation ~$8.5B (2022) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/sumup). Flatpay called Denmark's fastest company to reach unicorn status and targeting 300% growth in 2026 to €400–500M ARR (CEO statement) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3067856). Nory raised a $37M Series B in Sept 2025 led by Kinnevik, about $62.6M total — [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/16/nory-closes-37m-series-b-to-automate-restaurant-operations-with-ai/)
- Price changes: Epos Now acknowledged "recent pricing changes" in May 2026 after a customer reported a rise from £40 to £153/month; Epos Now Payments is now cited at 1.5% vs 1.7% on older sites; Odoo's Custom price rose for 2026 while Standard stayed flat; Dojo discounted EPOS Plus from £25 to £11.99 for new customers from 24 Aug 2026 — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/eposnow.com?page=7); [Business Expert](https://www.businessexpert.co.uk/payment-processing/epos-now-review/); [Skysize](https://www.skysize.io/blog/guides-5/how-much-does-odoo-cost-in-2026-the-complete-breakdown-31); [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/uk/merchant-accounts/dojo-review)

### Inferences
- The free-POS model depends on card volume; in a market where most sales are cash or informal credit (Lebanon), it does not transfer, which protects a modest subscription price — but it also means lebanon-POS cannot use the "free forever" hook unless something else (credit-tab collection, supplier ordering, payments via a local partner) funds it.
- Payment players are moving into software (Dojo EPOS at £11.99, Revolut POS free, Flatpay POS kits, Vectron bundling Shift4), compressing software prices from below; the direction of travel in Europe is toward software at €0–30 where payments are attached.

### Gaps
- Systematic 2023 → 2026 price history per vendor was not found; only the isolated changes above.
- Company-reported revenue splits (payments vs software) for Lightspeed, Dojo, Teya, Epos Now: not found.
- Flatpay funding amounts/valuation and Dojo's Spain/Italy expansion: search not completed.

## Key question 5 — Customer complaints per vendor

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints are (1) contract lock-in and mid-term price rises (Epos Now most clearly, Flatpay and Lightspeed to a lesser degree), (2) frozen funds and unexplained account closures at payment-led providers (SumUp, Zettle, Square), and (3) surprise fees (Dojo, sunday). Overall Trustpilot scores were mostly not retrievable; only review counts and themes were.

### Cited Findings
- Epos Now (25,200+ Trustpilot reviews): May 2026 reviews describe a plan rising from £40 to £153 overnight, fees added mid-contract, charges continuing after a paid cancellation fee, refunds pending since April 2026; Capterra's review summary says most reviewers who mention cancellation report problems (ongoing charges, ignored requests, restrictive terms). Many reviews also praise individual support staff and ease of use — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/eposnow.com?page=7); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/152638/Epos-Now/)
- Dojo: reported 4.3/5 on Trustpilot (third party, unverified); complaints about unexpectedly higher charges, payment-link fees, billing clarity, aggressive sales calls and weak help with hardware faults — [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/uk/merchant-accounts/dojo-review); [Expertsure](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/merchant-accounts/dojo-review/)
- SumUp (42,600+ Trustpilot reviews): held funds (one reviewer cites ~£30K inaccessible), extra "security" checks after requesting daily payouts; a third-party aggregator (labelled paid advertisement) shows 2.72/5 across 101 product reviews — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/sumup.com?page=5); [Smart Money People](https://smartmoneypeople.com/sumup-reviews/products)
- Zettle (5,600 Trustpilot reviews): terminals that stop working, accounts frozen as "high risk" with resolution measured in months, accounts closed without explanation — [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/zettle.com?page=10)

- Flatpay (about 2,160–2,190 Trustpilot reviews): recent reviews mostly 5-star and often name the account manager; negatives include a terminal that stopped connecting for several days and "waste of money and time" on the POS (2024); German review warns of long contract terms — [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/flatpay.com?page=2); [trusted.de](https://trusted.de/flatpay)

## Key question 6 — Distinctive ideas worth copying for a low-cost market

### Takeaway
The transferable ideas are commercial rather than technical: in-person onboarding (Flatpay), a generous free tier with cheap single-purpose add-ons (Loyverse, Hiboutik), an all-in monthly rental that includes the hardware (Vectron), a paid tier that visibly lowers another cost (SumUp, myPOS), and paying the switcher's exit fees (Dojo).

### Cited Findings
- Field sales and in-person onboarding: Flatpay onboards customers in person and its 5-star reviews often name the account manager; it reached unicorn status faster than any Danish company — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/16/flatpay-rings-up-47m-to-target-smaller-merchants-with-simple-payment-solutions); [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/flatpay.com?page=2); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3067856)
- Free core + $5 add-ons: Loyverse gives POS, loyalty, KDS, customer display and multi-store free and charges $5/month per store for each extra (sales history beyond 31 days, employee management), with 14-day trials and no contract — [Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/pricing)
- Free plan with paid reporting/API: Hiboutik's base is free; statistics, accounting export and API cost €9.90–12.90/month; extra shops €9.90 each — [Capterra FR](https://www.capterra.fr/software/129660/hiboutik); [independant.io](https://independant.io/avis/hiboutik/)
- Hardware included in one monthly fee: Vectron SMART rents the till with payment, cloud and service for €29.90–39.90/month — [Vectron press](https://www.vectron-systems.com/fileadmin/Content/Unternehmen/PR-IR/News/2026/Vectron-POS_8-2026-06-22.pdf)
- Cheap one-off starter kit: SumUp POS Lite tablet + reader for £290–349 with free software — [SumUp POS Lite](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/payment-solutions-made-simple/pos-lite/)
- Subscription that buys a lower fee: SumUp Payments Plus (£19 → 0.99%), myPOS Plus/Pro, Dojo's £39.99 flat fee covering the first £3,999 of card turnover — [SumUp vs Zettle](https://www.sumup.com/en-gb/compare/sumup-vs-zettle/); [myPOS AT fees](https://mypos.com/en-at/pricing-and-fees/fees); [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/uk/merchant-accounts/dojo-review)
- Instant settlement as a default feature: myPOS settles instantly even on the €0 plan; Teya advertises next-day settlement — [myPOS AT fees](https://mypos.com/en-at/pricing-and-fees/fees); [Teya pricing](https://teya.com/pricing)
- Buying out the competitor's contract: Dojo covers up to £500 (Fix) or £3,000 (Flex/Pro) of a switcher's exit fees — [Business Expert](https://www.businessexpert.co.uk/payment-processing/dojo-review/)
- Aggressive introductory software price: Dojo EPOS Plus at £11.99 instead of £25 for new customers and new locations — [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/uk/merchant-accounts/dojo-review)
- Short contracts as a selling point: Dojo 30-day rolling option; Tabology 14-day trial with no card; Lightspeed 14-day free cancellation window — [Business Expert](https://www.businessexpert.co.uk/payment-processing/dojo-review/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/154530/BarTab/pricing); [Expertsure](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/epos-systems/lightspeed-review/)
- Table tabs and pre-orders in a free POS: Revolut POS — [Revolut in-person](https://revolut.com/business/in-person/)
- Built-in AI assistant at no extra charge: Square AI — [Square press](https://squareup.com/ca/en/press/square-ai)
- Cautionary: charging the end customer a convenience fee (sunday) produced press backlash and a lawsuit — [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2487241)

### Inferences
- Best fit for lebanon-POS: (1) Loyverse/Hiboutik-style pricing — a usable free or very cheap single-till tier, with $5-style add-ons for reports history, staff, extra branches — sits naturally under the $15–35 list price; (2) Flatpay-style in-person setup, which suits a market where merchants distrust online sign-up; (3) a Vectron-style "till + software for one monthly fee" bundle using a low-cost Android tablet; (4) a written no-lock-in and no-price-rise promise aimed at the Epos Now complaint pattern.
- "E-receipt and loyalty by card" and SumUp instant payouts were named in the brief but no source in this session described them, so they are not asserted here.
