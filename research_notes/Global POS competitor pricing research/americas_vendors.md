## 1. Per-vendor pricing: software plans, hardware, processing rates, contracts, add-ons

### Takeaway
US list prices for software cluster at $0, $49–$69, and $149–$189 per location or terminal per month, but the software price is not where vendors earn: card processing (about 2.3%–2.6% + 10–15¢ in person) and hardware financing carry the economics, and "free" hardware is paid for with a higher card rate and a multi-year term. lebanon-POS at $15–35 per till sits below every paid US plan and above only Loyverse (free core, $5–$25 add-ons per store) and the Latin American acquirers that give software away to win card volume.

### Inferences
- Paid entry plans in the US start at $45–$69 per terminal or location (Aloha Cloud $45, Square Plus $49, POS Nation $49, KORONA $59, Lavu $59, Toast $69, TouchBistro $69, Lightspeed Restaurant $69), so lebanon-POS at $15–35 per till is roughly a quarter to a half of the US floor. It matches Latin American software-only prices (Alegra $14.99–$29.99, Fudo $35–$45), which are the more relevant benchmark.
- Add-ons are where US bills grow: one Square KDS screen ($20–$30) or one Toast handheld licence ($50) costs as much as a whole lebanon-POS till.
- Loyverse is the direct price threat: a single-store shop needing staff logins and inventory pays about $50/month, or $0 if it does without them.
- Several vendors (Toast, Genius, Rain, SkyTab, Chowly, iFood Pago) hide prices behind quotes. lebanon-POS's public price list is a differentiator against that group.

## 2. Features: offline, tabs and credit, loyalty, QR, kitchen display, delivery, inventory, staff, AI, lending, APIs, multi-currency

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 feature race in the US is about AI agents bundled at no extra charge (Toast IQ, Square's Managerbot/Squaremates) and paid labour and marketing modules on top; the search results gave almost nothing on offline mode, house accounts or multi-currency, which are lebanon-POS's core claims and so need a dedicated follow-up.

### Inferences
- Both market leaders now give the AI assistant away and charge for the modules it drives (Toast IQ Team at $60 + $9 per employee). A basic "ask your sales data" assistant is becoming table stakes rather than a paid tier.
- Square's "undo by asking" and its MCP/CLI release are cheap ideas to copy: reversible agent actions, and an open API that lets a shop owner's own AI tool read their data.
- Latin American products compete on local compliance (electronic invoicing) and local payment rails (QR, instalments) rather than on restaurant depth. The Lebanese equivalents are dual-currency pricing and customer credit tabs.

## 3. Business model: revenue mix, ARPU, and why payments lock-in matters

### Takeaway
The large US vendors are payments companies with software attached: 82% of Toast's 2025 revenue and 66% of Lightspeed's fiscal 2026 revenue came from transactions, hardware is sold at a loss, and software list prices are kept low because card fees pay for them. A vendor in Lebanon that cannot earn card fees must cover all cost from the subscription, so it cannot copy "$0 software" or "free hardware" offers.

### Inferences
(All arithmetic below is mine, from the cited figures.)
- Toast 2025 revenue mix: fintech 82% ($5.04B / $6.15B), subscription 15%, hardware and services 3%. Fintech revenue is gross of interchange, so its share of gross profit is lower than 82%.
- Toast per location: ARR $2,047M / 164,000 ≈ $12,500 a year ≈ $1,040 a month; subscription revenue $936M / 164,000 ≈ $5,700 a year ≈ $475 a month (year-end location count, so slightly understated). A restaurant on the "$69" plan pays Toast far more than $69 in software once add-ons are counted.
- Lightspeed fiscal 2026 mix: transaction 66%, subscription 30%, remainder (about $41M, 3%) hardware and other.
- Clover: revenue per merchant ≈ $3.3B / 910,000 ≈ $3,600 a year ≈ $300 a month (mixes a Fiserv figure with an analyst's merchant count). Value-added services ≈ $0.83B ≈ 25% of Clover revenue on the analyst's split.
- Shift4's spread of 60+ basis points on volume means a restaurant taking $50,000 a month in cards yields about $300 a month in net payment revenue, roughly ten times a lebanon-POS till subscription. This is why SkyTab can list software at $29.99 with subsidised hardware.
- Why lock-in matters: the vendor's payback depends on years of card volume, so contracts add auto-renewal, per-month termination charges (Toast, as alleged), non-cancellable leases and processor-locked hardware (Clover). Those terms are a consequence of the subsidy.
- Implication for Lebanon: with no payment revenue, lebanon-POS's $15–35 per till must cover support, hosting and acquisition on its own. It should not subsidise hardware or offer a free tier that relies on later payment income. The upside is that it has no need for lock-in terms, and can sell month-to-month with public prices as a trust advantage. Any future income beyond subscription has to come from paid modules (the Toast/Square add-on pattern) rather than a percentage of sales.

## 5. Ideas worth copying for a low-cost emerging market, and things that only work in the US

### Takeaway
Copy the packaging ideas (a free or cheap core with a few clearly priced add-ons, public prices, month-to-month terms, a bundled AI helper with undo) and the Latin American habit of building around local payment and compliance realities; do not copy anything funded by card fees, or the US-specific payroll and labour modules.

### Cited Findings
- Loyverse keeps the core free and sells four flat add-ons per store ($5 sales history, $9 integrations, $25 inventory, $25 staff) — [Loyverse pricing](https://loyverse.com/en-us/pricing)
- Square collapsed separate Retail/Restaurant/Appointments subscriptions into three plans per location in October 2025, calling it simplification — [Square press release](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/unified-pricing-and-packaging); [Square: thinking behind new plans](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/inside-square/pricing-plans)
- Square prices kitchen screens and kiosks per device and staff scheduling per employee, with the first five staff free — [Square for Restaurants pricing](https://squareup.com/us/en/point-of-sale/restaurants/pricing)
- Owner.com sells month-to-month with no cancellation fee, as a selling point against contract vendors — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10002488/Owner/pricing/)
- Toast and Square include their AI assistants at no extra cost; Square's can reverse its own actions on request and exposes an MCP server and CLI — [Toast AU](https://pos.toasttab.com/au/products/toast-iq); [Square press](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/managerbot-open-beta); [Square press](https://squareup.com/us/es/press/introducing-squaremates)
- Shopify removed the per-location POS charge for its largest customers (20 POS Pro locations included in Plus) — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/news/article/shopify-plus-bundles-20-free-pos-pro-locations-killing-the-per-location-tax)
- Annual-billing discounts of roughly 15%–18% are standard (Lightspeed $109 → $89; $179 → $149), and Lightspeed reports a growing share of customers choosing annual plans — [NerdWallet (affiliate)](https://www.nerdwallet.com/reviews/small-business/lightspeed-retail-pos); [Lightspeed FY2026 results](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/news/lightspeed-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2026-financial-results-and-provides-outlook-for-fiscal-2027/)
- Lavu gives hardware free on an annual plan — [UpMenu (competitor)](https://www.upmenu.com/blog/best-restaurant-pos-systems)
- Latin American acquirers sell the terminal for a small one-off price (MXN 199–499), charge no monthly rent, and earn on the card rate — [Tiendanube](https://www.tiendanube.com/blog/mx/cual-es-la-mejor-terminal-punto-de-venta-mexico/); [Mercado Pago MX](https://www.mercadopago.com.mx/herramientas-para-vender/lectores-point)
- Siigo partnered with a bank (Davivienda) to bundle POS with a free interoperable QR for SMEs — [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/siigo-y-davivienda-forman-alianza-para-ofrecer-siigo-pos-y-qr-interoperable-gratis-en-colombia/)
- US-specific modules: Square Payroll ($35 + $6 per person), Toast payroll (about $90 + $9 per employee), Toast IQ Team ($60 + $9 per employee) — [Square for Restaurants pricing](https://squareup.com/us/en/point-of-sale/restaurants/pricing); [Merchant Maverick (affiliate)](https://www.merchantmaverick.com/toast-pricing-guide); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/toast-launches-iq-team-to-help-restaurants-manage-labor-costs/)

### Inferences
Worth copying:
- A small number of flat, per-store add-ons on a cheap core (Loyverse), instead of quote-only add-ons (Toast).
- Per-device pricing for extra screens (kitchen display, kiosk) and a free allowance of staff seats before per-employee charges (Square).
- An annual plan at roughly 15%–18% off, which improves cash flow without a lock-in clause.
- Month-to-month, no cancellation fee, stated on the pricing page (Owner.com, Square).
- A bundled "ask your data" assistant with reversible actions, and an open API or MCP endpoint.
- A bank or wallet partnership that gives merchants a free QR payment option without the POS vendor touching the money (Siigo/Davivienda), adapted to Lebanese wallets.
- Building around the local money reality the way Latin American products build around instalments and e-invoicing; for Lebanon that is dual-currency and credit tabs.

Works only in the US (or only with payment revenue):
- $0 software and $0 hardware funded by a higher card rate, and the contracts needed to recover that subsidy.
- Payroll and tax filing, tip pooling and tipping screens, and labour-law scheduling tools, which depend on US payroll rules and card-based tipping.
- Embedded lending repaid from card settlements (Square Loans, Toast Capital, Lightspeed Capital), which needs the vendor to sit in the payment flow.
- Percentage-of-sales pricing of any kind.

Pricing traps to avoid:
- Fees charged to the merchant's customers without merchant control (Toast's 99-cent fee).
- Auto-renewal with per-month termination charges; hardware that cannot be reused elsewhere.
- Quote-only add-ons that make the real bill several times the headline price (Toast's roughly $475/month subscription revenue per location against a $69 list price, by my arithmetic in section 3).
- Conflicting or frequently changing price lists: several vendors show different prices on different pages, which is itself a source of distrust.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence was found on which of these ideas have actually worked in low-income markets; the copy/avoid lists are my inferences from US and Latin American pricing.
- Tipping-screen and tip-pooling features were not researched (search budget exhausted); their US-only status is background judgement, not a cited finding.
- Loyverse usage in Latin America (installed base by country) was not found.
