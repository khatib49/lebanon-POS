## 1. Gulf/Levant vendors: plans, prices, payments, funding

### Takeaway
Foodics is the regional restaurant benchmark at roughly SAR 228–765 per month (USD 61–204) per bundle in Saudi Arabia, with reported prices varying a lot by source and date; Saudi retail/accounting-led POS (Rewaa, Qoyod, Daftra, Wafeq, Zoho, Geidea) clusters at about SAR 39–275 per month (USD 10–73). lebanon-POS's $15–35 per till sits at the bottom of the Gulf range and overlaps the accounting-led POS add-ons, not Foodics.

### Inferences
- The Foodics Saudi figures form three incompatible sets (228/414/704; 392–423/742/1,133; 199/375/417). The 199/375/417 set matches UAE AED prices exactly, so it is probably a currency mislabel. The other two may differ by date, VAT (15%) or device count. A report writer should present a range (roughly USD 60–110 for the entry bundle) and not a single number.
- Gulf pricing is per branch or per register with annual prepayment discounts of about 8–25% and 1–2 year contracts (Rewaa, Sapaad, Foodics, Geidea). Monthly cancel-anytime is the exception (Geidea, Syrve claim it).
- Payments companies (Geidea) undercut pure software vendors on POS subscription (SAR 145–160) because they earn on card fees. A software-only entrant cannot match that subsidy.
- A $15–35 per till price would be about 25–60% of Foodics Starter and about equal to the Qoyod/Zoho/Daftra add-on level.

## 2. Egypt, Turkey and North Africa

### Takeaway
Egypt's "POS" market is led by payment acquirers (Paymob, Fawry, Geidea, Kashier) charging about 2.75% + EGP 3 with no monthly fee, with Foodics and Daftra supplying software; Turkey is a fiscal-device market where restaurant software is cheap (Menulux from ₺459 per month; SambaPOS a one-time licence of roughly USD 245–339) but must work with certified cash registers. North Africa evidence is thin beyond Chari in Morocco.

### Cited Findings

**Egypt**
- Paymob: standard rate 2.75% + EGP 3 per transaction, weekly settlement, no monthly or subscription fees (online pricing page); its POS page advertises daily settlement (secondary report). — [Paymob pricing](https://paymob.com/en/pricing); [Paymob POS](https://paymob.com/en/pos-solution)
- Kashier: reported to publish the same 2.75% + EGP 3, with 14% VAT on the fee and no charge on refunded or failed transactions (secondary source, not verified on Kashier's site). — [Startupik](https://startupik.com/payment-gateways-for-startups-in-egypt/)
- Fawry, Geidea and OPay publish no Egyptian merchant rate card; a competitor blog lists Fawry at 2.75% + EGP 3 with 5–10 day settlement (unverified). — [Startupik](https://startupik.com/payment-gateways-for-startups-in-egypt/); [Dodo Payments blog](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/paymob-alternatives)
- Fawry partnered with NARD and with Crystal Mind on integrated POS and merchant payment solutions. — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/571857.aspx); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/north-africa/egypt-fawry-crystal-mind-partner-to-launch-integrated-merchant-payment-solutions-404463)

**North Africa**

## 3. Sub-Saharan Africa

### Takeaway
Sub-Saharan "POS" is payments-first: the terminal is cheap or deposit-based and the vendor earns on transaction fees (South Africa about 2.0–2.75%, Kenya 0.55%, Nigeria 0.5% capped at ₦100 for agents), with software free or bundled. Subscription POS software is a secondary product (Yoco Pro R499, Lightspeed about R700, Bumpa from ₦15,000 per quarter).

### Cited Findings

## 6. Lebanon presence and regulatory barriers to entry

### Takeaway
No evidence was found of Foodics, Rewaa or Marn selling in Lebanon; the regional products visible there are Loyverse (self-serve) and Odoo (through at least five local partners). The main barrier in the other direction is tax-authority integration: Saudi ZATCA Phase 2 now reaches businesses above SAR 187,500 turnover, Jordan requires real-time JoFotara clearance for all invoices, Egypt is expanding B2C e-receipts, and Turkey requires certified fiscal devices.

### Cited Findings
- Foodics: no Lebanon-based reseller found; Foodics runs a reseller partner programme. — [Foodics](https://foodics.com/)
- Loyverse is recommended for single-location cafés in a Lebanese POS guide (written by a competing Lebanese vendor, Voxire) and appears in a Lebanon POS listing. — [Voxire](https://voxire.pages.dev/blog/best-pos-systems-lebanese-restaurants-2026/); [SoftwareSuggest Lebanon](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/point-of-sale-pos-software/lebanon)
- Odoo partners in Lebanon: Azkatech (Gold, Hazmieh; one reference includes POS), Tecomsa (Gold, Badaro), NavyBits (Silver), Circosoft (Silver, Ain Mreisse), ANALYB (Bsalim). — [Azkatech](https://www.odoo.com/partners/azkatech-1740990); [Tecomsa](https://www.odoo.com/customers/tecomsa-sal-2245530); [NavyBits](https://www.odoo.com/customers/navybits-1148678); [Circosoft](https://www.odoo.com/partners/circosoft-17039461); [ANALYB](https://www.odoo.com/partners/analyb-2656944)
- Saudi Arabia (ZATCA Phase 2 "integration"): wave 23 covered turnover above SAR 750,000 (deadline 31 Mar 2026); wave 24 above SAR 375,000 (deadline 30 Jun 2026); wave 25, announced 24 Jul 2026, covers turnover above SAR 187,500 (≈ USD 50,000) in 2025 with a deadline of 1 Feb 2027. ZATCA gives at least six months' notice per wave. Simplified (B2C) invoices need a QR code with mandatory fields. — [EY wave 25](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1705-saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/07/saudi-arabia-e-invoicing-mandatory-for-taxpayers-with-revenue-above-sar-187500.html); [EY wave 24](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2023-saudi-arabia-announces-24th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration); [Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en-sa/tax-and-reporting/which-zatca-wave-am-i-in-deadlines-and-what-to-do)
- Jordan (JoFotara): phase 2 took effect 1 Apr 2025; e-invoicing is mandatory for B2B, B2C and B2G, invoices must be cleared in real time to be valid, and POS/ERP must integrate; SME onboarding continues from 2026; small shops, crafts and bakeries under set limits are excluded. — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/07/17/jordans-e-invoicing-phase-2-mandatory-compliance-begins-april-1-2025/); [ClearTax](https://www.cleartax.com/jo/jordan-e-invoicing); [EDICOM](https://edicomgroup.com/blog/jordan-prepares-to-launch-the-electronic-invoice)
- Egypt (ETA e-receipt, B2C): Decision 281/2025 required newly listed taxpayers to integrate POS or ERP from 15 Sep 2025; a further Decision 361/2025 is listed for Nov 2025; B2B e-invoicing has been mandatory for VAT-registered businesses since 2023. Integration needs an e-signature certificate (Egypt Trust). — [Sovos](https://sovos.com/regulatory-updates/global-vat/egypt-tax-authority-extends-e-receipt-obligations-for-b2c-transactions/); [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/08/09/egypt-expands-e-receipt-mandate-for-b2c-transactions-starting-september-2025/); [Daftra](https://www.daftra.com/en/egy-electronic-invoice/?trk=SEO_EN_HUB_Menu)
- Turkey: Communiqué No. 593 (8 May 2026) allows e-documents to be issued directly from new-generation cash registers (YN ÖKC); e-Adisyon is mandatory for businesses individually notified by the revenue administration (Communiqué 509). Blog sources disagree on dates and penalty amounts. — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/05/27/turkey-enables-e-document-issuance-via-new-generation-cash-registers/); [GİB Communiqué 509 text](https://cdn.gib.gov.tr/api/gibportal-file/file/getFile?objectKey=MEVZUAT_TEBLIGLER%2FUNIVERSAL%2F2026%2FMEVZUAT_TEBLIGLER_2026_VukTeb509_Guncel.pdf); [RobotPOS](https://www.robotpos.com/blog_new/e-adisyon-gecisi-2026-mevzuat-donanim-pos-tam-rehber)
- Nigeria: one-principal rule for POS agents from 1 Apr 2026 (affects payment agents, not software). — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/moniepoint-vs-opay-agent-2026-which-is-better-for-pos-business-in-nigeria/)

### Inferences
- With wave 25, nearly every VAT-registered Saudi shop a newcomer could sell to will need Phase 2 integration by February 2027, so ZATCA integration is a precondition for entering Saudi Arabia, not an add-on.
- Jordan's real-time clearance conflicts directly with an offline-first design: an offline sale cannot be legally final until cleared. This needs a queue-and-clear design and a legal check before entering Jordan. (Inference; the sources state the clearance rule, not its effect on offline POS.)
- Turkey's certified-device regime makes it the hardest market for a web app; Egypt and Jordan are API integrations; Sub-Saharan markets found here have no comparable e-invoicing barrier in the sources, but payment integration (M-PESA, card acquirers) is the local requirement.

### Gaps
- Official ZATCA list of compliant e-invoicing solution providers and how a vendor gets on it: not retrieved.
- Whether Foodics, Rewaa, Marn or Geidea have Lebanese customers: no evidence either way.
- UAE e-invoicing timetable and Kenya eTIMS, Nigeria e-invoicing rules: not searched.
