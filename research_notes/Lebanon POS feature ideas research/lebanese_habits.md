## WhatsApp and social media

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is close to universal in Lebanon and is used to run businesses and even as the main news channel; social-media reach is ~70–78% of the population. Hard numbers on "ordering via WhatsApp" or on Instagram/TikTok per platform could not be retrieved, but the WhatsApp Business Platform is available for Lebanon (priced in Meta's "Rest of Middle East" group, roughly 3.4 US cents per marketing message per one third-party table).

### Cited Findings
- 4.02 million social media user identities in Lebanon in January 2025 = 68.9% of the population (DataReportal cautions identities are not unique people) — [DataReportal Digital 2025: Lebanon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-lebanon)
- 4.58 million social media user identities in October 2025 = 78.1% of the population — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Lebanon](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-lebanon)
- Instagram: about 2.5 million users (2024 estimate, via an aggregator citing DataReportal; not verified at source) — [dig.watch Lebanon](https://dig.watch/countries/lebanon)
- **[older]** 84% of Lebanese adults used WhatsApp in a 2018 Pew survey, the highest of 11 emerging economies surveyed; 98% of ages 18–29 vs 60% of ages 50+; WhatsApp beat Facebook and Instagram as the most used platform — [Pew Research, Nov 2019](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2019/11/19/protests-in-lebanon-highlight-ubiquity-of-whatsapp-dissatisfaction-with-government/)
- A 2026 nationwide LAU study found 59% rely on WhatsApp channels and groups as a primary news source, ahead of other social media (41%) and TV (38%) — [Al-Fanar Media, June 2026](https://al-fanarmedia.org/2026/06/whatsapp-silence-and-lebanons-information-war/)
- A UNDP study describes WhatsApp in Lebanon as used "to organize daily life, run businesses, and keep abreast of news" (qualitative, no share of businesses given) — [UNDP WhatsApp study, 2024](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2024-03/undp_whatsapp_study.pdf)
- WhatsApp Business Platform pricing: Meta prices per message by market and category; Lebanon sits in the "Rest of Middle East" rate group (with Bahrain, Iraq, Jordan, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Yemen) — [ManyChat help](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/14281380243740); [Meta pricing docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- One third-party rate table lists Lebanon at about USD 0.0341 per marketing message and USD 0.0091 per utility and per authentication message, with service (customer-initiated) conversations free. The column order was inferred and the rate-card date is not stated, so treat as indicative only — [sent.dm pricing guide](https://docs.sent.dm/docs/guides/whatsapp-pricing)
- A 2025 peer-reviewed study of Lebanon's food and beverage sector states that these businesses "heavily rely on social media" for marketing, brand awareness and loyalty, and studies ads, e-word-of-mouth and influencers — [MDPI Administrative Sciences 15(7):278](https://mdpi.com/2076-3387/15/7/278)
- **[anecdotal]** Lebanese food creators have large followings, e.g. one creator cited with 606k TikTok and 238k Instagram followers — [The961](https://www.the961.com/most-popular-lebanese-food-influencers/); ranked directories of Lebanese food influencers exist (vendor lists, unverified) — [Modash](https://www.modash.io/find-influencers/lebanon/food), [Heepsy](https://www.heepsy.com/top-instagram/food/lebanon)

### Inferences
- With WhatsApp near-universal and service conversations free, WhatsApp-first flows (receipt by WhatsApp, order confirmation, "your table is ready", loyalty balance) cost little; only merchant-initiated marketing blasts cost about 3 cents each, which is material for a small shop sending thousands.
- WhatsApp channels/groups being the top news source suggests customers will accept business broadcast channels (daily dish, offers) as a normal medium.
- Instagram is the storefront for restaurants and shops; features such as shareable menu/offer cards and an "order via link in bio" page fit existing behaviour.

### Gaps
- No retrievable 2023–2026 figure for WhatsApp penetration (the Pew 84% is 2018), nor for TikTok/Facebook users per platform (DataReportal pages blocked).
- No survey quantifying the share of Lebanese businesses taking orders via WhatsApp.
- No Lebanon-specific evidence found on Google Maps/reviews usage, nor on influencer fees or measured sales effect.
- Official Meta rate for Lebanon not confirmed at source.

## Dining and café culture

### Takeaway
Eating out is a core social ritual: long shared mezze meals, Sunday family lunches, argileh, and one person paying for the whole table as a matter of generosity (karam). Tipping is around 10–15% in cash even where a service charge appears; seasonal peaks are Ramadan, Lent, year-end holidays and the summer expatriate season.

### Cited Findings
- **[anecdotal]** Sunday lunch: families go to ancestral villages or to coast/mountain restaurants and spend hours over mezze, arak and argileh; described as a way to keep family ties — [Culinary Backstreets](https://culinarybackstreets.com/?p=12110); Zahle's Berdawni riverside is singled out for Sunday mezze-and-arak lunches — [Sunheron](https://sunheron.com/blog/beverages/traditional-alcoholic-beverages-lebanon/)
- **[anecdotal]** Mezze at home is typically three or four dishes; restaurant mezze can run to 20–60 dishes — [Wikipedia: Lebanese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_cuisine)
- **[anecdotal]** Bill-paying: Lebanese often take turns paying the whole table's bill rather than each paying their own, and "fight" for the bill; tied to the value of karam (generosity); restaurants let diners linger and do not bring the bill unasked — [365 Days of Lebanon, 2015](https://365daysoflebanon.com/2015/12/12/the-fight-for-the-right-to-pay/); [Islands.com](https://www.islands.com/2268261/lebanese-restaurant-rules-american-tourists-break-first-10-minutes/); [SBS Cultural Atlas](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/lebanese-culture/lebanese-culture-etiquette)
- **[anecdotal]** Younger generations in Arab communities are said to be moving away from the bill battle toward splitting — [Arab America](https://www.arabamerica.com/navigating-the-bill-battle)
- Tipping: guides conflict (10%, 12–15%, about 15%); a service charge on the bill often goes to the establishment rather than staff, so an extra cash tip is customary; some venues add 15% automatically; valets and porters expect small bills — [The961 tipping guide](https://www.the961.com/ultimate-guide-tipping-in-lebanon/); [CIA travel facts](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/static/1ddc11fe6b36ede48609884027ec1deb/LE-travel-facts.pdf). L'Orient Today ran a piece titled "5 percent, 20 percent, nothing at all: what tips do people give in Lebanon?" (not readable here) — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1474237/5-percent-20-percent-nothing-at-all-what-tips-do-people-give-in-lebanon.html)
- Argileh: adult waterpipe smoking prevalence 39% (nationally representative AUB study); 94% of waterpipe users smoke at home and 30% in cafés; per-bowl prices are much higher in cafés, implying high margins for hospitality venues — [Economics for Health / AUB report](https://www.economicsforhealth.org/files/research/1110/research-report-waterpipe-smoking-in-lebanon-finalized-.pdf)
- **[older]** Indoor smoking is banned by Law 174 (2011) but a 2019 report called smoking inside restaurants, cafés and bars "almost universal"; a 2012 EY study for the restaurant syndicate estimated the ban could cost up to USD 280 million in revenue — [Jordan Times/AFP](https://jordantimes.com/news/region/lebanese-bubble-anger-over-hookah-tax); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/smoking-ban-leaves-lebanese-fuming-flna983244)
- Ramadan: restaurants sell fixed-price iftar set menus (2023 examples USD 10, 28, 35, 45 per person; typical contents lentil soup, fattoush, dates, main, katayef/knafeh) and suhoor venues are ranked by media — [Beirut.com 2023 offers](https://www.beirut.com/l/65062); [Lebanon Traveler](https://www.lebanontraveler.com/en/magazine/where-to-experience-iftar-ramadan-in-lebanon/); [The961 suhour](https://www.the961.com/7-best-rated-suhour-places-lebanon/)
- Lent: chains publish dedicated fasting menus (Kababji "Holy Season Fasting Menu", Zaatar w Zeit combos, Roadster vegan options, fish "tawouk") — [The961](https://www.the961.com/restaurants-lent-friendly-menus/); [Beirut.com](https://www.beirut.com/en/?p=39065)
- Sector scale: restaurant-sector employment fell from about 150,000 before 2019 to about 60,000, recovering to roughly 100,000 by early 2025 — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/news-bulletin-reports/858651/lebanons-culinary-charm-and-hospitality-restaurant-sector-gears-up-to/en). **[older]** 8,500 restaurant establishments in 2019 per the syndicate — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/press/693830/lebanons-restaurant-institutions-have-decreased-in-number-since-2019-r/en)
- Holiday-season restaurant bookings were put at 60–80% by syndicate president Tony Ramy (date unclear) — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/news-bulletin-reports/896521/holiday-dining-in-lebanon-restaurants-busy-catering-thrives-on-events/en)
- Delivery: Toters offers "Toters Rewards" points redeemable for offers, discounts and free meals — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/toters/id1015006220); Forbes Middle East profiled it with 500,000+ customers and an USD 18 million Series B led by IFC (June 2022) **[older]** — [Forbes Middle East](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/50-most-funded-startups-2022/toters/); other apps in Lebanon's top-5 rankings include noknok and talabat — [Sensor Tower Q4 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-food-delivery-services-units-lb-63da96fbe1714cfff1c1e5a1)
- **[anecdotal]** An App Store reviewer describes Toters as a "virtual monopoly" over Beirut delivery — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/toters-totrz/id1015006220?l=en); Statista projects Lebanon restaurant-delivery revenue at USD 30.74 million in 2025 — [Statista](https://statista.com/outlook/dmo/online-food-delivery/meal-delivery/restaurant-delivery/lebanon)

### Inferences
- "One payer for the table" is the default, so split-bill tooling matters less than in Europe; more useful are fast single-payer checkout, discreet "pay before the bill arrives" (pre-settling at the counter to win the bill fight), and an easy way for a host to pre-pay or open a tab for guests. Split-by-item is a secondary feature aimed at younger groups.
- Because the service charge is not trusted to reach staff, a transparent, separate tip line (cash or digital, clearly going to staff) could be valued by both customers and staff.
- Argileh is a high-margin line item sold per head and often refilled; a POS should treat it as a first-class item (per-head, head changes, timing).
- A seasonal menu switch (iftar set menu with per-person pricing and sunset-time booking; siyami/Lent filter or tag; Sunday lunch large-table mezze sets) maps directly onto observed practice.

### Gaps
- No household data on eating-out frequency or spend; no share of café revenue from argileh.
- No verified Toters market share, order volume or commission rate; no evidence on which delivery features customers like most.
- Wedding-season economics not researched with a source.
- No evidence found that Qlub operates in Lebanon; its published market lists (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Türkiye and others) omit Lebanon — [Qlub](https://qlub.io/ae/en/pay-at-table), [Stripe case study](https://stripe.com/au/customers/qlub)

## Diaspora

### Takeaway
Remittances are USD 5.8–6.9 billion a year depending on the source (about 18–31% of GDP), mostly arriving as cash carried by hand and spent on basic needs. Expatriates dominate summer arrivals. Services for paying from abroad for family in Lebanon exist but are small and gift-focused, which leaves room for merchant-level "pay from abroad" features.

### Cited Findings
- Diaspora size is contested: 3–4 million is often cited; people of Lebanese descent are put at 4–14 million or 15.4 million (vs about 6 million in Lebanon); first-generation migrants only were estimated at 885,000 in 2014 **[older]**; "no reliable source" exists — [Wikipedia: Lebanese diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_diaspora); [IFPO/OpenEdition](https://books.openedition.org/ifpo/13224)
- World Bank: USD 6.7 billion in 2023 = 30.7% of GDP, third-highest ratio in the world — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1419163/lebanon-received-67-billion-in-remittances-in-2023-world-bank.html); [Kataeb.org](https://en.kataeb.org/articles/expatriates-remittances-to-lebanon-up-4-to-67-billion-in-2023)
- World Bank: USD 5.8 billion in 2024, down 13.4%, = 17.7% of GDP (17th highest) — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1449753/world-bank-lebanon-received-58-billion-in-remittances-in-2024.html); [Kataeb.org](https://en.kataeb.org/articles/global-remittance-growth-contrasts-with-lebanons-134-drop-in-2024); a different denominator gives about 15% — [Business News](https://www.businessnews.com.lb/cms/Story/StoryDetails/12450/Expatriates-remittances-up-or-down)
- Conflict: BDL puts 2024 at USD 6.8–6.9 billion (up about 6%) versus the World Bank's USD 5.8 billion; BDL puts 2025 at USD 6.4 billion, down 7.6% — [Business News](https://www.businessnews.com.lb/cms/Story/StoryDetails/12450/Expatriates-remittances-up-or-down); [Byblos Bank Lebanon This Week 915](https://byblos.byblosbank.com/files/Library/Assets/Gallery/Publications/LebanonThisWeek/Lebanon this Week 915.pdf). No World Bank 2025 figure was found.
- Channels: about 70% of inflows during the crisis came through informal channels such as cash carried in person; a 2021 estimate was 70% cash, 30% money-transfer companies, about 0% banks; the average cost of sending to Lebanon was 11% vs a 6% global average — [UNDP executive summary, June 2023](https://global-prod.acquia.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2023-06/executive_summary-_remittances_report_0.pdf); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1339933/pockets-full-of-cash-but-not-lira.html)
- Use: remittances are now primarily spent on basic and survival needs (food, rent, electricity bills), where before the crisis they went more to health and education — [UNDP press release](https://www.undp.org/lebanon/press-releases/undp-launch-report-increasing-role-and-importance-remittances-lebanon)
- Whish Money: partnered with Mastercard Move (August 2025) for transfers to 50+ countries, and with Visa (May 2025); its CEO claims 90% of local digital transactions (company claim) — [Mastercard newsroom](https://www.mastercard.com/news/eemea/en/newsroom/press-releases/en/2025-1/august/whish-money-collaborates-with-mastercard-move-to-bring-seamless-cross-border-payments-to-lebanon); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1450086/whish-money-pioneer-of-digital-transactions-and-global-fintech-integration.html)
- Summer: tourist arrivals fell 32% from 1.67 million (2023) to 1.13 million (2024); in summer 2025 expatriates were "the overwhelming majority" of visitors, with 17,000–18,000 daily airport arrivals (peaks of 20,000) — [This Is Beirut](https://thisisbeirut.com.lb/articles/1324151/summer-2025-lebanon-reclaims-its-suitcases-and-its-traffic-jams); [This Is Beirut](https://thisisbeirut.com.lb/articles/1321581/lebanon-sees-modest-summer-travel-rebound)
- Existing diaspora-to-family services: Presentail (gifts, flowers, sweets ordered from abroad for delivery in Lebanon; started by expats with four vendors) — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/presentail-a-gift-and-flower-platform-for-lebanese-expats-1.1638792238169); Wasleh (free platform matching travellers with people sending items home) — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=47433); iShip US-to-Lebanon parcel forwarding with pay-on-delivery by family **[vendor page]** — [iShip](https://iship-ish7anli.lovable.app/from-usa)
- **[older]** During 2020–2021, expatriates carried medicines and essentials in suitcases and shipped aid to relatives — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.6162145)
- Gift card and digital wallet transactions surged in Lebanon during the 2024 conflict — [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/gift-card-and-digital-wallet-transactions-surge-in-lebanon-amid-israel-conflict/)

### Inferences
- A relative abroad paying a specific local merchant directly (prepaid credit at the family's grocer, butcher, pharmacy or bakery; settling a monthly tab) would bypass an 11% transfer cost and the cash-carrying habit, and matches the "basic needs" use of remittances.
- Summer (roughly June–September) and year-end holidays are when diaspora spending is physically present; features for visitors (international cards, USD pricing, English/French UI, gift vouchers bought on departure for family) should be timed to those seasons.

### Gaps
- No verified data on summer visitors' spend per head, nor on OMT/Western Union market shares.
- Could not confirm whether Toters supports ordering/gifting for someone else with a foreign card, or whether Lebanese online supermarkets accept foreign cards.
- No World Bank 2025 remittance estimate found.

## Gifting and generosity customs

### Takeaway
Pay-it-forward through local food shops has real precedent in Lebanon (a "suspended manoushe" scheme modelled on caffè sospeso), and community feeding through bakeries recurs in every crisis. Evidence is journalistic rather than statistical.

### Cited Findings
- **[older]** "Man2ouche 3al Raf" (2021): customers at participating bakeries pay for a "pending" manoushe for someone in need; the founder cited Italy's caffè sospeso as the model — [Al Jazeera, June 2021](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/6/4/as-economic-crisis-wears-on-lebanese-cant-even-afford-manoucheh)
- **[older]** "Manousheh 4 Lebanon" Christmas campaign (2021) funded food parcels for struggling families — [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/flatbread-poor-lebanese-charity-initiatives-christmas); a Tripoli bakery baked free manoushe all night for protesters (2019) — [The961](https://www.the961.com/this-tripoli-bakery-is-baking-manoush-all-night-feeding-the-protesters-for-free/)
- 2026: a displaced grandmother runs a free bakery in a Beirut waterfront tent settlement, with a donated oven and volunteers bringing flour — [AP via WTOP, May 2026](https://wtop.com/world/2026/05/a-displaced-lebanese-grandmother-feeds-thousands-of-compatriots-who-fled-war/)
- MEDCO's fuel loyalty scheme lets members donate points to an NGO — [MEDCO help centre](https://medco-78852.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/23416906010396)

### Inferences
- A built-in "suspended item" button at the till (pay for a manoushe, coffee or bread bundle for someone else, with a visible counter) and a "donate my points" option both have local precedent and suit the karam ethic.
- Gift vouchers sent by WhatsApp link are a natural fit, especially for diaspora buyers, but demand is inferred, not measured.

### Gaps
- No data on gift-voucher usage in Lebanon, and no confirmation that Man2ouche 3al Raf is still running.
- No sourced detail on occasion gifting (Eid, Christmas, births, engagements) volumes.

## Loyalty

### Takeaway
Points programmes are familiar across supermarkets, fuel stations and delivery apps, mostly app-based with QR scanning, cashback-style points and gamified promotions. Evidence on what customers actually respond to is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Spinneys Lebanon: app purchases earn loyalty points redeemable in store or online; programme dates to 2007 **[older]**, with co-branded corporate cards — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1518229592); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/spinneys-launches-corporate-loyalty-cards-for-companies-across-lebanon-k3uehont)
- Le Charcutier (Aoun Group) has an app for its 19-branch chain; the listing seen did not mention points — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760022832)
- MEDCO "MEDCOins": points on fuel and shop purchases by scanning the receipt QR; redeemable for fuel, car wash, oil change or convenience-store items, or donated; app runs daily promotions and a spin-the-wheel game — [MEDCO help centre](https://medco-78852.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/23416906010396); [My MEDCO app](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/my-medco/id1126018669)
- Coral: points per fuel purchase plus e-wallet and QR payment — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/coral-lebanon/id1600860923); TotalEnergies launched its first Lebanese loyalty card, "My Pass", in 2025 — [TotalEnergies Lebanon](https://lb.totalenergies.com/en/business/totalenergies-card); WEEV offers cashback points spendable "like cash" across partners — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1466038606)
- "SAM Loyalty" stores several loyalty cards in one app, implying customers hold multiple cards — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/sam-loyalty/id6449619585)
- Toters Rewards: points redeemable for discounts and free meals — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/toters/id1015006220)
- **[older]** An academic study surveyed 225 Lebanese loyalty-card holders on programme effectiveness (findings not retrieved) — [IGI Global](https://igi-global.com/chapter/evaluating-the-effectiveness-of-loyalty-programs-in-the-retail-sector/238547)

### Inferences
- The prevailing pattern (points as cash-equivalent, QR on receipt, gamified spin, donation option) sets customer expectations; an independent café or shop could match it with a phone-number-based stamp card needing no app download.
- Cash-like value probably matters more than status tiers in a price-sensitive, dollarised market (inference, not evidenced).

### Gaps
- No Lebanon data on stamp cards in cafés, on bank card rewards in 2025, on Carrefour/MAF's Lebanese programme, or on redemption rates and customer preferences.

## Trust issues customers face

### Takeaway
Pricing transparency and exchange-rate display at the till are regulated and still actively disputed; banknote quality and counterfeit scares are a daily friction; card surcharges are now banned and card use is rising fast.

### Cited Findings
- Consumer Protection Directorate: 1,133 complaints via its digital platform and about 1,400 violations recorded since the start of 2025; complaints can be filed by app — [Kataeb.org](https://en.kataeb.org/articles/why-food-prices-keep-rising-in-lebanon-despite-a-stable-dollar)
- 2023 dollar-pricing decision: supermarkets may price in USD; the approved rate must be displayed on shelves, at the entrance and at the tills; invoices must show totals and VAT in lira plus date, time and rate; customers may pay in lira or dollars — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/economy/690020/minister-of-economy-issues-dollar-pricing-decision/en); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/mena/lebanon/2023/03/01/dollars-or-pounds-lebanons-supermarkets-begin-pricing-goods-in-dollars)
- Supermarkets were accused of not displaying the rate and of keeping prices high after the rate fell; the ministry sought powers to seal stores without a court order — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/news-bulletin-reports/692961/ministry-of-economy-cracks-down-on-price-gouging-by-supermarkets-and-g/en)
- Old or worn USD notes are refused by shops, changers and banks ("Maak ghayra?" — do you have another one?); even supermarkets refuse damaged notes; BDL later declared such refusal illegal — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1411659/banks-and-businesses-are-rejecting-old-bad-usd-notes-in-lebanon-why.html); [This Is Beirut](https://thisisbeirut.com.lb/articles/1320187/bdl-orders-institutions-to-accept-old-us-dollar-notes)
- Counterfeit USD 50 scare, December 2024: banks and changers briefly stopped accepting 50s; afterwards over 80% of shops, supermarkets and fuel stations accepted them again and most retailers updated bill-counter software — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1439172/counterfeit-50-bills-allegedly-circulating-in-the-lebanese-market-what-we-know.html); [This Is Beirut](https://thisisbeirut.com.lb/articles/1304025/counterfeit-50-bills-an-issue-nearing-resolution); [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/economy/823850/lebanese-syndicate-of-money-changers-concerns-over-counterfeit-50-bill/en)
- Card surcharges: BDL forbids merchants from adding fees for card or electronic payment — [Forvis Mazars FS Alert 05-2026](https://www.forvismazars.com/lb/fr/content/download/1294066/file/FS%20Alert%2005-2026%20-%20fees%20regulation%20on%20electronic%20card%20payments%20.pdf); merchant fees on local cards capped at 1.25% for fuel stations, bakeries, supermarkets, hospitals and pharmacies (restaurants not named) — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1505868/bdl-caps-merchant-fees-on-local-card-payments-at-125-in-various-sectors.html)
- Card payments rose 130% in the first eight months of 2026 year on year — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1547050/card-payments-surge-130-in-lebanon-bdl-outlines-measures-to-curb-cash-economy.html); restaurants' card share reportedly rose from 15% to 35% (date unclear) — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=51325)
- **[older]** In the crisis years some restaurants refused cards or added 20–30% surcharges, and supermarkets accepted cards for only 50% of a bill — [Beirut.com](https://www.beirut.com/en/?p=63756); [The961](https://www.the961.com/supermarkets-accepting-card-payments-to-only-50/)

### Inferences
- A receipt showing both currencies, the rate used, its timestamp and VAT is a legal requirement in retail as well as a trust feature; a customer-facing display of the rate and running total removes the most common till dispute.
- Recording note denominations/condition and prompting for counterfeit checks on 50s and 100s, and giving change in a stated currency mix, address real friction.
- With surcharges banned and fees capped, "card accepted, no extra fee" is now a selling point merchants can display.

### Gaps
- No breakdown of complaints by type (exchange rate, receipts, surcharges); whether the 2023 pricing decision remains in force unchanged was not confirmed.
- No statistics on counterfeit incidence.

## Small-business owners

### Takeaway
Lebanese business is overwhelmingly family-owned and small, but almost nothing citable was found on owners' attitudes to subscriptions, accountants, remote ownership or staff theft.

### Cited Findings
- Family businesses are estimated at 85–95% of the private sector (multiple secondary estimates; a 2006 figure attributes 1.05 million of 1.24 million jobs to them **[older]**) — [The Business Year](https://thebusinessyear.com/article/mom-pop/); [AUB ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.aub.edu.lb/items/78c93c1a-b1f8-4e7c-aa4d-3f86321a63cc); [WEE Portal](https://weeportal-lb.org/news/family-businesses-secure-80-employment-opportunities-lebanon-and-yet-do-not-benefit-state)
- An LAU policy brief states Lebanon lacks reliable national data on family businesses — [LAU policy brief](https://sb.lau.edu.lb/images/Policy%20Brief%20-%20Driving%20Family%20Business%20Revival_Final.pdf)
- **[older]** SMEs are about 95% of Lebanese enterprises (Ministry of Economy, 2014) — [Ministry of Economy](https://www.economy.gov.lb/media/10311/launching-of-the-sme-strategy-december-16-2014.pdf)
- Power outages and inconsistent internet hinder adoption of digital technologies — [US Commercial Service country guide](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/lebanon-digital-economy)
- Investors moved money into hospitality because real estate and banking had stalled (syndicate member, June 2024) — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/lebanon-news/778891/michel-aji-reveals-to-lbci-restaurant-business-is-booming/en)
- High electricity cost is flagged by the restaurant syndicate as a main burden — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/lebanon-news/781607/maya-noun-to-lbci-lebanons-restaurants-ready-for-tourist-season-emphas/en)

### Inferences
- Family ownership plus emigration suggests many owners or co-owners are partly abroad; remote owner dashboards and WhatsApp daily summaries are plausible high-value features, but this is inference only.
- Unreliable power and internet make offline-first operation a baseline requirement rather than a nicety.

### Gaps
- No sources found on: owners living abroad, reliance on WhatsApp groups for staff/supplier coordination, willingness to pay subscriptions, accountant relationships, or staff-theft concerns. These need primary interviews.

## Languages and dialect

### Takeaway
No direct evidence was found on Lebanese users' preferred app language. Research confirms dialect and Arabizi dominate informal writing while MSA is seen as formal, and that Arabizi spelling is not standardised.

### Cited Findings
- MSA is reserved for formal, academic and religious settings and "often perceived as inaccessible or overly formal for daily use"; Arabizi is described as the dominant written form in informal settings — [MT Summit 2025 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.mtsummit-2.4/)
- **[older]** A study of Beirut Twitter found Lebanese Arabizi used less than expected, attributed to low prestige and globalisation; consonant spellings are fairly standard, vowels vary — [University of Texas thesis](https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/handle/2152/72420)
- Arabizi is mainly used in instant messaging and rarely for long text — [arXiv survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.12622)
- Toters brands itself bilingually in the App Store ("Toters توترز") — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/toters/id1015006220)

### Inferences
- A sensible default is English and Arabic UI with French optional, a light Lebanese-flavoured tone in Arabic customer messages rather than stiff MSA, and search that tolerates Arabizi spellings of item names (e.g. man2oushe, 3asir). This is a design inference, not a measured preference.

### Gaps
- No survey on preferred app/UI language in Lebanon, on French usage in apps, or on reactions to dialect vs MSA copy. Needs user testing.
