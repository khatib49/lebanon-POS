## 1. What did the leading POS vendors launch or highlight in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
Every major vendor shipped the same three-step AI sequence in 2025–2026: (1) a chat-with-your-data assistant for owners, (2) proactive insights pushed at login or on mobile, (3) "agents" that do a job (build the staff schedule, write the marketing campaign, place supplier orders). Outside AI, the launches that recur across vendors are handheld/all-in-one terminals, kitchen display, server upsell prompts, supplier/procurement tools, invoice scanning, embedded lending and faster tip/payout tools.

### Inferences
- The table-stakes AI feature for 2026 is an owner-facing assistant that answers questions about the shop's own sales data, on mobile. Toast, Square, Lightspeed, Shopify, Foodics and Poster all have one. A new POS without it will look dated; one with it in Arabic/French/English would be differentiated in Lebanon.
- The next step vendors are monetising is agents that do work: scheduling (Toast charges separately and calls it its fastest-growing product), marketing campaigns, supplier ordering. Scheduling and supplier ordering map well to small Lebanese restaurants; marketing agents depend on a customer database.
- Supplier-side features are an under-noticed cluster: Square Order Guide, Petpooja Best Match/price comparison, Petpooja and Poster invoice scanning, Lightspeed AI OCR for inventory. For Lebanon (volatile supplier prices, USD/LBP invoices) "photograph the supplier invoice, update stock and cost" looks high-value.
- Sales presets per order type (dine-in/takeaway/delivery), product combos, and server upsell prompts are cheap to build and were highlighted in 2025 releases.
- Embedded lending is a major revenue line for Toast, Square and Foodics but depends on processing card payments and a lending partner; hard to replicate in Lebanon's cash-heavy market in the short term.

## 2. What do merchants value most, miss most, and complain about?

### Takeaway
Reviews reward ease of use and fast set-up above any advanced feature; complaints cluster around money and lock-in (contracts, hidden and rising fees, proprietary hardware), weak or rigid reporting, and poor support. Survey data says operators put money into loyalty and digital marketing first.

### Cited Findings
- On G2, Square POS scores 95% for ease of use and 94% for ease of set-up, well above category average; in G2's small-business POS category "ease of use" is the top-mentioned pro (85 mentions) — [G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/best-retail-pos-systems)
- G2 retail POS satisfaction: card payments 93%, receipts 90%, barcode scanning 87%, price adjustments 87% — [G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/best-retail-pos-systems)
- A Shopify POS reviewer on G2 cites limited offline capability (cannot reliably process transactions without internet); this rests on few reviews — [G2 Shopify POS reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/shopify-shopify-pos/reviews)
- Clover reviewers on G2 are frustrated by limited customisation, especially reporting and central control — [G2 Clover review](https://www.g2.com/survey_responses/clover-review-813632)
- Common complaints: long contracts (2–3 years, auto-renewal, early-termination fees), processing fees and fees appearing after signing (PCI fees, rate increases on "locked" rates), poor support, unstable systems — [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/pos/common-pos-problems-in-restaurants); [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/-99-problems-the-true-cost-of-your-point-of-sale-system); [StartupOwl Toast review](https://startupowl.com/reviews/toast)
- Clover complaints (as listed by competitor Toast): poor customer service, proprietary hardware lock-in, reliance on paid third-party apps, mandatory monthly fees — [Toast blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/clover-pos-reviews) (competitor source)
- Square complaints (from competitor Owner.com): rising costs, limited customisation, clunky reporting — [Owner.com](https://www.owner.com/blog/square-alternatives) (competitor source)
- A merchant-services blog summarising Reddit says owners advise picking a system staff can learn easily and using wired connections rather than Wi-Fi only for stability; no thread links given — [merchant blog](https://merchantbancardawp-aef7e0dxhbgwd4ap.westus2-01.azurewebsites.net/?p=6889) (unverifiable secondhand)
- Square 2025 restaurant report: 71% of restaurants planned to increase loyalty/rewards investment; 83% of leaders with loyalty programmes reported ROI through larger orders and repeat visits; about one-third agreed kiosks and KDS increase productivity (vendor survey) — [Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/operating-your-business/restaurant-trends)
- 2025 Restaurant Technology Outlook (Restaurant Business/NRN, 550 operators): 46% most likely to spend tech money on digital marketing; 37% named loyalty programmes when asked about dissatisfaction — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/digital-marketing-remains-restaurants-top-tech-priority)
- SpotOn-commissioned survey (2024, based on 2023 data; older): 58% said handhelds gave faster service, 48% said higher average checks — reported in search summary via [QSRweb](https://www.qsrweb.com/news/operators-report-correlation-between-profitability-increases-technology-deployments) (attribution uncertain)
- Deloitte (June 2025): 82% of restaurant executives plan to increase AI investment next fiscal year — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-how-ai-is-revolutionizing-restaurants.html)

### Inferences
- Positioning opportunities from the complaint list: month-to-month pricing with no termination fee, transparent flat pricing, runs on any hardware (the PWA already gives this), exportable data, and reachable local support (WhatsApp). These are "features" in buyers' eyes.
- Reporting is where incumbents are weakest in reviews: flexible/custom reports and multi-branch central control are worth investing in; a chat-with-data assistant partly answers the "clunky reporting" complaint.
- Loyalty is the best-evidenced growth feature by operator intent and self-reported ROI, and operators are also often dissatisfied with their current loyalty tool, so a simple one (phone-number based, WhatsApp-delivered) is a strong candidate.
- Handheld order-taking has survey support for speed and check size; a PWA on staff phones delivers this without hardware.

### Gaps
- No first-hand Reddit threads (r/restaurateur, r/smallbusiness) were retrievable; search returned vendor/competitor content instead. Capterra, Trustpilot and app-store reviews were not reached.
- Almost all complaint evidence is US-centric and filtered through competitors' blogs.
- No data found on which features merchants in emerging/cash-heavy markets miss most.

Voice ordering should be deprioritised, more so given Lebanese Arabic/French/English code-switching.

## 4. Loss-prevention and anti-theft features owners value

### Takeaway
The consistently recommended controls are manager approval and reporting for voids/comps/discounts, blind cash close-outs, per-employee logins (ideally biometric), and linking POS events to camera footage. Evidence of effect is vendor-supplied; no controlled studies were found.

### Cited Findings
- Widely repeated National Restaurant Association estimate: internal employee theft causes 75% of inventory shortages and about 4% of restaurant sales (old, undated figure repeated across vendor articles) — [Toast blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/how-to-prevent-employee-theft-at-your-restaurant)
- Blind close-out (staff count the drawer without being shown the expected amount) is described by Toast as the best preventive measure against short-ringing — [Toast blog](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/how-to-prevent-employee-theft-at-your-restaurant); an operator using it so the cashier does not know the drawer total until shift end — [Hospitality Technology](https://hospitalitytech.com/putting-stop-shrink)
- Void tracking: compare void and comp reports with inventory — [Lightspeed blog](https://lightspeedhq.com/blog/restaurant-fraud-theft); require manager sign-off for voids and comps — [Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/?p=39340)
- Camera integration is described as the strongest mitigation: POS sends amount, items, tender, employee ID and event type (void, return, no-sale, discount) to the video system so managers can search e.g. "void over $20" — [FoodTec](https://foodtecsolutions.com/blog/2023/03/23/employee-fraud-the-3-most-common-occurrences-and-what-you-can-do-about-it) (2023, older); [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/operations/restaurant-operations/preventing-employee-theft/)
- NCR Restaurant Guard (pattern-based fraud alerts) cited as moving one restaurant group from -3% loss to +7% gain (vendor's own study) — [Fast Casual](https://www.fastcasual.com/articles/using-technology-to-stop-restaurant-theft/)
- Biometric login replaces shared PINs — [Touch Dynamic](https://www.touchdynamic.com/point-of-sale-security-3-ways-your-pos-reveals-employee-theft/); Clover added biometric authentication in Jan 2026 (see section 1, attribution uncertain)

### Inferences
- Beyond the already-planned void/discount audit log, candidates are: blind cash count at shift close with over/short report per cashier; "no-sale" drawer-open logging; manager PIN/remote approval for voids, refunds and discounts above a threshold; per-employee exception report (voids, discounts, refunds, deleted items before payment, reprints) ranked against peers; owner push alerts for unusual events; and a time-stamped event feed that can be matched to CCTV.
- Full camera integration is heavy; a cheap version is an exportable event timeline with timestamps, or a snapshot from a tablet camera at void/no-sale events.
- In Lebanon's cash-heavy, dual-currency setting, cash-drawer controls per currency (separate USD and LBP expected vs counted) are likely worth more than in card-heavy markets.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of loss reduction per feature. No 2024–2026 survey of which loss-prevention features owners rank highest.
- Nothing found on AI anomaly detection results in SMB POS.

## 5. Onboarding features that cut set-up time

### Takeaway
AI menu import from a photo, PDF or CSV became standard in 2025–2026 (Square, Lightspeed, Teya, Joe Coffee, Revolut), always with a human review step. Retail-side equivalents are AI OCR for product entry and supplier-invoice scanning. No evidence was found for shared barcode product databases.

### Cited Findings
- Square for Restaurants: set-up path to upload a file or photo of the existing menu, plus import from another platform — [Square help](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6424-create-menus-with-square-for-restaurants)
- Lightspeed Restaurant (O-Series): AI menu importer accepts PDF, CSV and images; user reviews results before they enter the catalogue; "may take a few minutes" — [Lightspeed help](https://o-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/51562172008987)
- Teya: AI import from PDF or photo; PDFs most accurate; handwritten or stylised menus less reliable — [Teya help](https://help.teya.com/hc/en-gb/articles/49062567716625-Importing-your-menu-with-AI)
- Joe Coffee Menu AI: CSV preferred, PDF and images accepted — [Joe help](https://support.joe.coffee/hc/en-us/articles/43246376009108-Menu-AI)
- Revolut POS "Snap & Sell": photo of menu, AI extracts names, descriptions, prices — [Revolut help](https://help.revolut.com/help/more/revolut-pro/accept-payments-with-revolut-pos/question-how-do-i-upload-catalog-with-snap-sell-on-revolut-pos-pro/)
- A vendor blog notes the review pass takes longer than the import itself — [Duck Hub](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/ai-menu-import)
- Lightspeed Retail AI OCR automates product data entry for new inventory (section 1); Petpooja and Poster scan supplier invoices (section 1); Lightspeed "Smart Items" speeds menu building; Odoo 19 sales presets for dine-in/takeaway/delivery (section 1)
