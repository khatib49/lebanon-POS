# Build trust features first, spectacle last

Lebanon-POS will be loved for the unglamorous things: a customer-credit (dayn) book that fills itself from sales and sends the balance by WhatsApp, a till that balances in dollars and lira, a nightly WhatsApp summary for the owner, and a setup so easy that a shopkeeper can do it from a photo of a price list. The evidence supports those ideas far better than the eye-catching ones. Of the ideas already on the table, some deserve to be built early in a simple form (dayn with WhatsApp reminders, the owner's nightly report, photo-to-menu import, a "suspended coffee" counter, single-shop loyalty, and recording a relative's payment toward a tab); some are good but belong later (hybrid QR table service with a typed waiter bell, the argileh coal button, smart restock as a reorder list, chat-with-your-data in Arabic); some need a licensed partner or a lawyer before any code is written (pay from the phone, Tfaddal vouchers bought online or from abroad, recharge and bill-pay resale); and some should be dropped or reshaped (neighbourhood shared loyalty points, a full 3D/AR "4D" menu, and the "end the bill fight" framing). The single biggest constraint is the central bank: since January 2026, any company that holds, pools or passes on other people's money needs a licence with minimum capital of roughly $280,000 to $560,000, so Lebanon-POS must stay a piece of software that records and initiates payments and never touches the money. One honest warning applies to everything below: the researchers could not open full web pages, so almost every figure comes from search-result summaries, most restaurant numbers are American and vendor-reported, and nothing measures what Lebanese owners or diners actually want. Treat this as a well-informed shortlist to test in the 10–20 pilot shops, not as proof.

## Free ledgers win shops, but nobody pays for a ledger alone

The clearest lesson from other cash-heavy countries is about customer credit. Digital credit-book apps in India, Pakistan and Indonesia attracted millions of shopkeepers with one simple loop: record the debt, send a reminder, collect. OkCredit described its model as "record, remind and payments" and said merchants sent more than **250,000 WhatsApp reminders a day** ([OkCredit](https://medium.com/okcredit/record-remind-and-payments-a-full-stack-model-that-ticks-all-the-right-boxes-for-merchants-7a465b01f418)). That is strong evidence that shopkeepers use this feature. It is not evidence that it speeds up repayment: the famous "collect 3X faster" line is Khatabook's own marketing with no method behind it ([Khatabook](https://khatabook.com/blog/use-khatabook-for-faster-returns)), and no independent study was found.

The business lesson is harsher. A free ledger has almost never paid for itself. Indonesia's Lummo went into liquidation ([DealStreetAsia](https://media.dealstreetasia.com/stories/indonesia-lummo-liquidation-360554)), Nigeria's Kippa shut its payments add-on after losses and internal fraud ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/01/15/kippa-loses-millions-to-fraud/)), and OkCredit was still loss-making in its 2025 financial year ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/tiger-global-backed-okcredit-narrows-fy25-loss-by-34-to-inr-23-cr/)). The one visible subscription success, India's Vyapar, charges a small yearly fee for billing, stock and printing and includes the ledger for free ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/pricing-detail)). For Lebanon-POS this means **dayn belongs in the base product as the hook, and the paid plan should be justified by stock, reports, multiple tills and backup**.

Two design points follow from the same evidence. First, a pilot in which small businesses had to record their sales by hand found that only about half kept it up ([CEGA](https://cega.berkeley.edu/collection/digital-sales-and-inventory-data-to-assess-creditworthiness/)); Lebanon-POS avoids that trap if "on the tab" is simply one of the payment buttons at checkout, so the ledger writes itself. Second, OkCredit itself said a reminder is only "a bridge" and needs a way to pay; in Lebanon the reminder should state the exact balance in the currency of the debt and, later, carry a Whish or OMT payment link.

Offline selling and cheap receipt printing are treated as standard by the products small shops actually pay for ([Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/pricing-detail); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=712120)), and Lebanon's power and internet problems make them non-negotiable ([US Commercial Service](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/lebanon-digital-economy)). An Indian study adds a quieter point: small retailers resisted digital tools partly from **fear of tax exposure** and lack of trust ([IIM Bangalore](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/bitstream/2074/13997/1/Diatha_ACIS_2018.pdf)). Lebanese owners in a largely informal economy will likely feel the same, so saying plainly who can see a shop's data is itself a feature, and it matters for the accountant portal and the AI assistant discussed below.

Finally, reviews of the big global products reward ease above everything: Square scores **95% for ease of use and 94% for ease of setup** on G2, and complaints cluster around long contracts, creeping fees, locked-in hardware and weak reports ([G2](https://learn.g2.com/best-retail-pos-systems); [Expert Market](https://expertmarket.com/pos/common-pos-problems-in-restaurants)). Month-to-month pricing, a public price list, any-device hardware and data export are therefore things buyers count as features. These reviews are American and partly filtered through competitors' blogs, so they are a guide, not a measurement of Lebanese taste.

## The central bank decides which money ideas can be built

Several ideas on the table move money between people: paying from the phone, gift vouchers, diaspora top-ups, shared points, recharge resale. Their fate depends less on demand than on one rule. The central bank's Decision 13790 of 9 January 2026 created five licence categories for payment companies, including Category A (electronic money) and Category E (payment facilitators that collect electronic payments and pass them on) ([BDL Decision 13790](https://www.bdl.gov.lb/CB%20Com/Laws%20And%20Regulations/Basic%20Circulars/Decision_13790_EN%C2%A711334_3.pdf)). A law-firm summary puts the minimum capital at **LBP 50 billion (not less than about $558,660) for Categories A to D and LBP 25 billion for Category E** ([Melki Law Firm](https://melkilawfirm.com/article/lebanons-central-bank-issues-first-comprehensive-fintech-regulation/)). Lebanon also remains on the FATF grey list after the October 2026 review ([ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-october-2026-four-grey-list-exits-a-virtual-asset-review-and-a-new-focus-on-scam-compounds/)) and on the EU's high-risk list since August 2025 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/1184/oj/eng)), so banks and payment partners are in a tightening mood.

The working rule that follows is an interpretation, not legal advice: **Lebanon-POS is safe while money flows straight from the payer to the shop's own account and the software only records it; the moment Lebanon-POS collects, holds, splits or forwards money, it needs a licence or a licensed partner.** No Lebanese source was found saying whether a shop's own gift vouchers, store credit or loyalty points count as electronic money. The definition in Law 81 of 2018 ("electronic monetary units stored in electronic form") is broad ([Law 81](https://tahseen.ae/media/3197/e-transaction-law-lebanon-official.pdf)), so a lawyer must read the decision's exclusions before launch. One fragment of the decision also appears to say something about Categories A to D being "suspended until further notice"; the researchers could not read the surrounding sentence, and it needs checking.

**Dayn with WhatsApp reminders passes easily.** It is a record of what a customer owes one shop; no money moves through the software. WhatsApp is the right channel: a 2026 national study found 59% of Lebanese rely on WhatsApp channels and groups as a main news source ([Al-Fanar Media](https://al-fanarmedia.org/2026/06/whatsapp-silence-and-lebanons-information-war/)), and an automated WhatsApp "utility" message costs about **$0.009** against roughly **$0.32–0.35 for an SMS** ([SleekFlow](https://sleekflow.io/blog/whatsapp-business-price); [Plivo](https://www.plivo.com/sms/pricing/lb/)). A cheaper first version, which is a design suggestion rather than a research finding, is a button that opens the owner's own WhatsApp with the message already written. The weak spot is local evidence: grocers have extended more credit since 2019 ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/2368861/falling-cliff-lebanons-poor-borrow-buy-bread)), but nothing shows how they track it or how customers react to reminders, so wording and tone must be tested in the pilot.

**Diaspora top-up of a relative's tab is a real need with an awkward route.** Remittances were $5.8 billion in 2024 by World Bank count ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1449753/world-bank-lebanon-received-58-billion-in-remittances-in-2024.html)); about 70% arrived informally as carried cash, sending cost about 11% against a 6% world average ([UNDP](https://global-prod.acquia.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2023-06/executive_summary-_remittances_report_0.pdf)), and the money now goes mainly on food, rent and electricity ([UNDP](https://www.undp.org/lebanon/press-releases/undp-launch-report-increasing-role-and-importance-remittances-lebanon)). Paying the family's grocer directly fits that pattern. Demand, however, is inferred and unmeasured. The legally cleaner shape is a card payment from abroad that lands in that one shop's own merchant account against that one customer's tab, with no cash-out and no transfer between shops. That requires the shop to have an online card gateway, which a corner grocer usually lacks, and foreign-card fees are unpublished. Collecting abroad through a foreign company and sending the money on would make Lebanon-POS a cross-border money mover and should be treated as blocked. The sensible start costs nothing: when a relative sends money by Whish or OMT, which already deliver from abroad into a wallet or as cash ([Whish FAQ](https://www.whish.money/faq)), the shop records a tab payment marked "paid by" that relative, and both people get a WhatsApp receipt.

**Tfaddal vouchers split into a safe half and a risky half.** A voucher for one shop, paid to that shop, with the POS only recording what is owed, is "probably fine, unconfirmed". A Tfaddal balance usable across many shops, or a marketplace where Lebanon-POS takes the money and pays shops, is licensed activity. Local precedent is encouraging for the culture and thin for the product: Presentail and Gift On Click let expatriates pay online for gifts delivered in Lebanon ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/presentail-a-gift-and-flower-platform-for-lebanese-expats-1.1638792238169); [Gift On Click](https://giftonclick.com/en-lb)), but those are delivered goods, and no data on voucher use in Lebanon was found. "Suspended coffee" has the best precedent of all: in 2021 the "Man2ouche 3al Raf" scheme let bakery customers pay for a manoushe for someone in need, modelled on Italy's suspended coffee ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/6/4/as-economic-crisis-wears-on-lebanese-cant-even-afford-manoucheh)). A suspended-item button at the till, paid in cash to the shop with a visible counter, needs no payment partner and can ship early. Vouchers bought online from abroad should wait for a gateway and a lawyer.

**Neighbourhood shared loyalty points should be dropped for now.** No evidence of a successful shared scheme among small shops was found anywhere, and brand-funded promotion apps such as Morocco's WafR moved away from loyalty toward bill payments ([We Are Tech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/solutions/moroccan-fintech-wafr-turns-corner-stores-into-digital-finance-hubs)). Points that work like cash across unrelated shops look like electronic money, and someone has to settle between the shops. Loyalty inside one shop is a different story: 71% of restaurants in a Square survey planned to spend more on it and 83% of those with a programme reported a return (a vendor survey) ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/the-bottom-line/operating-your-business/restaurant-trends)), and Lebanese shoppers already know points from Spinneys, MEDCO fuel stations and Toters ([MEDCO](https://medco-78852.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/23416906010396); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/toters/id1015006220)). A stamp card keyed to a phone number, with no app to download, is the right first step; MEDCO's "donate my points" option is a nice touch to copy.

**Recharge and bill-pay resale needs a partner and will earn little.** Egypt's Fawry built a profitable business on small cuts from bills and top-ups paid through shops ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/04/20/how-fawry-built-a-profitable-fintech/)), but Fawry is a licensed network, and Kippa's attempt to bolt agency services onto shop software ended in closure. In Lebanon the rails already belong to OMT and Whish ([Whish fees](https://www.whish.money/fees-commissions)), collecting bills for others is licensed activity, the shop's margin is thin, and one report describes a move to shut down the parallel recharge market ([Libnanews](https://en.libnanews.com/alfa-touch-what-s-behind-the-recharges/)). Kenya shows what happens over time: average M-Pesa agent commission hit a record low as agents multiplied ([Business Daily Africa](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/rising-agent-numbers-cut-average-m-pesa-commissions-5536894)). For now, let shops sell recharge cards as ordinary items and track their commission; pursue a real integration only if OMT or Whish offers a reseller programme.

## At the table, guests accept QR for paying, not for replacing the waiter

**QR table ordering with a live bill** should be built as an optional fast lane, never the only way to order. A Technomic survey cited by the Wall Street Journal found 88% of diners would rather have a paper menu ([Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/paper-menu-qr-code)). The US National Restaurant Association's 2024 survey found people more open than that suggests (59% would read a menu by QR, 48% would order, 46% would pay), but with a steep age gap: **65% of millennials would pay by QR against 16% of baby boomers** ([NRA](https://go.restaurant.org/rs/078-ZLA-461/images/NatRestAssoc_TechLandscapeReport_2024.pdf?version=0)). In a country of long family lunches with grandparents at the table, forcing the phone would be a mistake. The gains vendors report (bills about 10–12% larger, minutes saved per table) all come from the vendors themselves ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Mobile-Order-and-Pay-Overview)), and none of it is Lebanese. A plain QR menu is also no longer special: local rival BIM POS already bundles one ([BIM POS](https://bimpos.com/blog/what-are-the-benefits-of-the-integrated-qr-code-menu-for-restaurants)). The value lies in what happens after the menu: reordering a round, seeing the bill grow, calling for something specific.

Two safeguards are essential. Items marked sold out at the till must vanish from the phone menu instantly, or guests order what the kitchen cannot make. And a table's QR code must work only while the waiter has that table open, with the first order confirmed by staff: in China, strangers kept ordering to a diner's table after she posted a photo showing its code ([China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202312/04/WS656dd404a31090682a5f15f7.html)).

**The waiter bell** has no independent proof of benefit, but it costs little once the QR page exists. Make it specific ("water", "bill", "coal") so staff arrive with the right thing, and record how long each request waited, which gives owners a service measure they have never had ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2018/restaurants-hospitality-service-call-buttons-restech/)).

**The argileh coal-change button** is the most original restaurant idea on the list. A national study put adult waterpipe smoking at 39%, with 30% of smokers using cafés, where prices per bowl are far higher than at home ([AUB / Economics for Health](https://www.economicsforhealth.org/files/research/1110/research-report-waterpipe-smoking-in-lebanon-finalized-.pdf)). Hookah-lounge software found abroad handles stock, flavour mixes and happy hours ([RPOWER](https://www.rpowerpos.com/Home/Market-Vertical/Hookah-POS)); none offered a coal request or a per-argileh timer. That gap is suggestive, not conclusive, because Arabic, Turkish and Russian products were not searched, and no one has asked Lebanese guests whether they want it. It is cheap to add to the bell: route "coal" to the argileh attendant's phone, start a timer when a head is served, and prompt staff to offer a fresh one.

**Pay from the phone** is technically possible and commercially dependent on partners. Card payments rose 130% in the first eight months of 2026 and the electronic share at restaurants reportedly went from 15% to 35% ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1547050/card-payments-surge-130-in-lebanon-bdl-outlines-measures-to-curb-cash-economy.html)). Stripe and PayPal do not serve Lebanese merchants, according to forum reports ([Webflow forum](https://discourse.webflow.com/t/country-not-found-in-country-list-for-business-address/118410)), so each restaurant needs its own agreement with a local provider; Whish publishes fees of 1% for websites and 3% for payment links ([Whish fees](https://whish.money/fees-commissions)), and a Whish collection interface exists, though its documentation was seen only as a third-party copy ([lebsol copy](https://lebsol.com/4del/Whish-Collect-Web-Service-Technical-Specification.pdf)). This corrects the earlier competitive research, which found no Whish interface at all. Google Pay arrived in June 2025 ([Visa](https://km.visamiddleeast.com/en_KM/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases/prl-24062025.html)); Apple Pay was not found to be available. Card surcharges are banned ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1505876/bdl-caps-commissions-on-certain-bank-payments-imposed-on-merchants.html)), so the payment page must never add a "card fee". The best-supported extra is a suggested-tip screen: Toast reports tips 3.8 percentage points higher with QR payment ([Toast](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-with-Toast-Pay)), and in Lebanon a service charge often goes to the house rather than staff ([The961](https://www.the961.com/ultimate-guide-tipping-in-lebanon/)), so a tip line that visibly reaches the waiter could win staff over. Those are vendor numbers.

**"End the bill fight" misreads the custom.** By the accounts found, which are travel and culture writing rather than surveys, Lebanese diners take turns paying for the whole table and compete for the honour as a matter of generosity ([SBS Cultural Atlas](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/lebanese-culture/lebanese-culture-etiquette); [365 Days of Lebanon](https://365daysoflebanon.com/2015/12/12/the-fight-for-the-right-to-pay/)). People do not want the fight ended; they want to win it. The feature that fits is paying the whole bill quietly from the phone before it reaches the table, or a host opening a tab in advance. Splitting by item suits younger groups ([Arab America](https://www.arabamerica.com/navigating-the-bill-battle)) and should be a secondary option.

**The 3D/AR "4D" menu is the weakest idea on the list.** Studies show 3D food images raise appetite and the intention to visit ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-022-00919-x); [WSU](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2026/02/18/augmented-reality-menus-may-help-restaurants-attract-customers/)), but the one restaurant experiment that tracked real spending, a Dutch master's thesis, found **no increase in order value** ([TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:0a814007-0d67-4ec0-9a7c-f789e8d80e1e)). The "+20%" sales claims are old vendor anecdotes ([Martech](https://martech.org/how-ar-is-whetting-restaurant-patrons-appetites/)). Each dish must be scanned individually, glossy and steaming food scans badly, and one guideline allows up to 10–15 MB per model ([KeyShot](https://manual.keyshot.com/?p=15938)), heavy for mobile data that costs about $3 per gigabyte ([ts2.tech](https://ts2.tech/en/state-of-internet-access-in-lebanon-from-fiber-optics-to-starlink-skies/)). "4D" has no accepted meaning, and the nearest real feature, prices that rise at busy times, provokes anger: more than half of diners in one survey called it price gouging ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/dynamic-pricing-customer-restaurant-dining-experience/713418/)). What works instead is scheduled menus and prices that customers already accept: happy hour, fixed-price iftar menus, and Lent fasting menus, all of which Lebanese restaurants run every year ([Beirut.com](https://www.beirut.com/l/65062); [The961](https://www.the961.com/restaurants-lent-friendly-menus/)). If 3D survives at all, it should be a rotatable view of five to ten signature dishes, sold as an optional extra and loaded only when a guest taps.

## For owners, the proven AI is import and questions, not voice or prediction

**Photo-to-menu import** is now standard: Square, Lightspeed, Teya and Revolut all turn a photo or PDF of a menu into a product list, always with a human review step ([Square](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6424-create-menus-with-square-for-restaurants); [Lightspeed](https://o-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/51562172008987); [Revolut](https://help.revolut.com/help/more/revolut-pro/accept-payments-with-revolut-pos/question-how-do-i-upload-catalog-with-snap-sell-on-revolut-pos-pro/)). Teya warns that handwritten and stylised menus read poorly ([Teya](https://help.teya.com/hc/en-gb/articles/49062567716625-Importing-your-menu-with-AI)), and no one has published accuracy for Arabic or mixed-script menus. So this idea is necessary rather than differentiating, and the quality of the review screen, with Arabic, French and English and prices in two currencies, is where to compete. Because the pilot is retail, the same tool should accept a supplier price list or a spreadsheet.

**The owner's nightly WhatsApp report** is cheap and fits what little is known: family firms are estimated at 85–95% of the private sector ([The Business Year](https://thebusinessyear.com/article/mom-pop/)), and big vendors now push daily insights to owners' phones. No source confirms that Lebanese owners are often away or abroad; that belief needs a few interviews. At about one cent per message, it is worth shipping regardless.

**Chat with your data in Arabic** follows a real trend: Toast, Square, Lightspeed, Shopify, Foodics and Poster each launched an owner assistant in 2025–2026. The one usage number is Square's: nearly **200,000 questions** in about four months of beta ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251008312312/en/Square-AI-Gains-New-Intelligence-Capabilities-Providing-Deeper-Business-and-Neighborhood-Insights-to-Square-Sellers)). Nothing shows owners keep using these assistants or earn more because of them. An assistant that understands Lebanese Arabic, including Arabic typed in Latin letters, would stand out, but it is only useful once a shop has months of sales and stock data, so it belongs after reports. Square limits its assistant to owners and admins ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-ai-open-beta)); copy that. Voice ordering, by contrast, should be avoided: McDonald's ended its test and Burger King reported customers driving off rather than talk to a bot ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/mcdonalds-ends-ai-drive-thru-ordering); [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/burger-king-rethinks-its-drive-thru-ai-strategy)).

**Smart restock** is right if it stays light. Companies that ran their own warehouses and trucks to restock small shops mostly collapsed or merged between 2022 and 2025; one analysis says the model "largely crumbled" ([WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2025/07/15/african-b2b-ecommerce-fintech-pivot/)). What survives is software: Square's Order Guide and Petpooja's supplier price comparison ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-releases-ai); [Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/procurement-cost-control/petpooja-ai-features-help-restaurants-decide-better/)). For Lebanon-POS that means a suggested reorder list built from sales, sent to the shop's existing distributors as a WhatsApp message or PDF, plus photographing a supplier invoice to update stock and cost. No deliveries, no stock on credit.

**The accountant portal has no evidence for or against it.** Nothing was found on how Lebanese shop owners work with accountants. The need is real in outline, because VAT returns and books are kept in lira, but the tax-fear finding above cuts the other way: some owners will not want an outsider seeing live sales. Start with an export the owner chooses to send (a VAT report in lira and a spreadsheet), add a read-only accountant login the owner can switch off, and build a full portal only if pilot accountants ask.

The research also surfaced features that are not on the current list and are better supported than several that are. For theft control, the consistent advice is a blind cash count (the cashier counts the drawer without seeing the expected total), manager approval for voids and refunds, and a per-cashier exceptions report ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/how-to-prevent-employee-theft-at-your-restaurant); [Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/?p=39340)); in Lebanon that means one count per currency. For customer trust, the 2023 pricing decision requires the exchange rate to be shown at the till and on the invoice ([LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/economy/690020/minister-of-economy-issues-dollar-pricing-decision/en)), the consumer directorate logged about 1,400 violations in 2025 ([Kataeb.org](https://en.kataeb.org/articles/why-food-prices-keep-rising-in-lebanon-despite-a-stable-dollar)), and shops and customers still argue over worn or suspect dollar notes ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1411659/banks-and-businesses-are-rejecting-old-bad-usd-notes-in-lebanon-why.html)); a customer-facing screen showing the rate and running total, and a prompt to check $50 and $100 notes, answer daily friction. For restaurants, simple prompts that remind staff to offer a drink or dessert produced about 6% larger orders in one Toast pilot ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2698584)), a single anecdote but a cheap feature. For pharmacies, tracking expiry dates by batch is what separates useful software from the rest, according to a vendor overview ([AVIXA Xchange](https://xchange.avixa.org/posts/7-pharmacy-pos-software-studios-solving-the-inventory-expiry-problem)).

## Conclusion

The pattern across all five research strands is that the ideas which feel most inventive are the least supported, and the ideas which sound dull are the ones shopkeepers elsewhere actually adopted. That is good news for a small team: the features with the best evidence (a self-filling dayn book, cash that balances, a WhatsApp report, a fast import) are also the cheapest to build and need no partner, licence or new hardware. The distinctive Lebanese touches worth keeping are the ones that ride on that base at almost no cost: a suspended manoushe counter, a coal button, paying quietly for the table, a relative's name on a tab payment.

The research changes the money features most. Lebanon-POS should think of itself as the record-keeper beside the payment, never the pipe the payment flows through, and should spend one session with a Lebanese fintech lawyer on vouchers, store credit and points before Phase 2 is designed. The remaining unknowns cannot be searched for; they sit in the pilot shops. Five questions will settle most of this list: how do you track dayn today and would you send a reminder; who else needs to see your numbers; do customers ask to pay by Whish or card; would a relative abroad pay your shop directly; and, for cafés, what do guests wave at the waiter for most.

## The prioritised list

Phases: 0 foundation, 1 selling MVP, 2 customers and cash control, 3 stock and reports, 4 smart restaurant, 5 growth.

### Add now / build early

1. **"On the tab" as a payment button, with a WhatsApp balance message** — the proven hook for small shops, and it needs no licence; keep it free. Phase 1 (simple), Phase 2 (reminders, statements).
2. **Blind cash count per currency, manager approval for voids and refunds** — the theft controls owners are consistently told to use. Phase 1.
3. **Exchange rate on the receipt and on a customer-facing screen; never a card fee line** — required by the pricing rules and removes the commonest till argument. Phase 1.
4. **Import from a photo, PDF or spreadsheet with a good review screen, plus ready-made shop templates** — setup ease is what reviewers reward most. Phase 1.
5. **WhatsApp receipt and nightly owner summary** — about one cent each and no printer needed. Phase 1 (receipt), Phase 2 (summary).
6. **Search that understands Arabic, English and Arabic typed in Latin letters** — matches how Lebanese people actually write. Phase 1.
7. **Month-to-month price, public price list, data export** — answers the top complaints about other POS products. Phase 0–1.
8. **Recharge cards as ordinary items with commission tracking** — captures today's habit with no partner. Phase 1.
9. **Stamp card by phone number, with "donate my points"** — single-shop loyalty is the best-supported growth feature. Phase 2.
10. **Tab payment marked "paid by" a relative, with a WhatsApp receipt to both** — serves the diaspora need using Whish, OMT or cash that already exist. Phase 2.
11. **Suspended item button with a visible counter** — local precedent, pure goodwill, cash to the shop. Phase 2.
12. **Prompt to check $50 and $100 notes and record the change given in each currency** — answers a real daily friction. Phase 2.

### Add later

1. **Reorder list sent to existing suppliers by WhatsApp or PDF** — the light version of restocking is the one that survived. Phase 3.
2. **Supplier invoice photo that updates stock and cost** — shipped by several vendors; saves typing. Phase 3.
3. **VAT and sales export for the accountant, then a read-only login the owner controls** — real need, unknown demand. Phase 3.
4. **Scheduled menus and prices (iftar, Lent, happy hour) and combos** — accepted by customers, unlike surge pricing. Phase 3–4.
5. **Chat with your data in Lebanese Arabic, owner only** — clear trend, unproven value, needs months of data. Phase 3 (basic), Phase 5 (full).
6. **Optional QR at the table: menu, reorder, live bill, typed bell with response times** — works only while the table is open, first order confirmed by staff, sold-out items hidden. Phase 4.
7. **Argileh coal button and per-head timer** — cheap, distinctive, sent to the argileh attendant. Phase 4.
8. **Staff prompts to offer a drink or dessert** — small but plausible gain. Phase 4.
9. **Expiry dates by batch for pharmacies** — the feature pharmacies need most. Phase 3 or 5.

### Needs a partner or legal check first

1. **Pay from the phone, with a suggested tip and "pay quietly for the whole table"** — each venue needs its own Whish or card gateway; money goes straight to the venue. Phase 4–5.
2. **Tfaddal voucher for one shop, sold at the till or online** — lawyer to confirm it is not electronic money; check expiry and refund rules. Phase 2 (at the till) once cleared, Phase 5 (online).
3. **Voucher or tab top-up paid by card from abroad** — needs a shop gateway that takes foreign cards; never route through a foreign company. Phase 5.
4. **Recharge and bill payment sold from the POS screen** — only as a sub-agent of OMT, Whish or a licensed distributor; expect thin margins. Phase 5.
5. **Stock on credit or loans based on sales data** — needs a licensed lender; not for now. After Phase 5.

### Drop or rethink

1. **Neighbourhood shared loyalty points** — no success found anywhere, and cash-like points risk being electronic money; do single-shop loyalty instead.
2. **A Tfaddal balance usable across many shops, or a gift marketplace that handles the money** — licensed activity needing minimum capital of roughly $280,000 to $560,000 by the law-firm figures.
3. **Full 3D/AR menu and the "4D" label** — the only real spending test found no gain; at most offer a handful of signature dishes as a paid extra in Phase 5.
4. **Prices that rise at busy times** — diners call it gouging.
5. **QR as the only way to order** — most diners prefer a paper menu, older guests most of all.
6. **"End the bill fight" as a bill-splitting pitch** — the custom is to win the bill, so sell "pay quietly"; keep splitting as a side option.
7. **Voice ordering, an online storefront, own delivery or warehouses, collecting money offshore** — each has a record of failure or a legal wall.
