# Win Lebanon's tills with offline-first trust

A new POS can compete in Lebanon, but not by advertising dual currency: almost every serious local vendor already claims USD/LBP support, so that is now the entry ticket, not the prize. The opening is elsewhere. Local vendors mostly hide their prices behind "contact us", lean toward restaurants, and sell through dealers and technicians, while imported products break on Lebanese realities (Foodics had to ship a Lebanon-only fix because ordinary orders in lira exceeded its transaction limit). The winning product is one a shop owner can install on their own Android phone or tablet, that keeps selling through power and internet cuts, that gets the money right (USD and lira cash in the same sale, correct change, two cash drawers that balance at night, VAT shown in lira at 89,500), and that tracks customer credit ("dayn") better than the notebook. Price it openly at roughly $15–35 per till per month with a free single-till tier to fight piracy and free apps, and collect in cash, Whish or OMT through local resellers. The Lebanese market alone is modest (likely 15,000–30,000 outlets that might pay, worth perhaps under $1M a year in subscriptions at realistic adoption) and volatile, with the World Bank forecasting a 6.4% economic contraction in 2026, so Lebanon should be treated as the proving ground and the Lebanese diaspora markets and the Gulf as the scale-up. The current phase plan is broadly right but should move cash control and customer credit into the first sellable release, build compliance and audit foundations into Phase 0, and narrow the beachhead to retail and counter-service cafés before full table-service restaurants.

## Dual currency is table stakes; the real gaps are price, setup and trust

The Lebanese market is crowded but old-fashioned. On the restaurant side, **BIM POS** (Dora, founded 2008, claiming 4,000+ installations in 23+ countries) and **Omega POS** (Beirut) are the long-established suites, with features like table management, split checks, delivery and loyalty ([Slashdot BIM POS](https://slashdot.org/software/p/BIM-POS/); [TrustRadius](https://tr-web.dev.trustradius.com/products/bim-pos-masterdine/details); [Omega POS](https://www.omegapos.com/)). Newer cloud entrants include **CeevTech** in Jbeil, a cloud restaurant POS with USD and LBP and Arabic/English in Basic, Pro and Ultra plans ([G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/ceevtech)), and **Voxire RTYLR**, which markets offline mode, daily rate sync and Toters/Talabat integration ([Voxire](https://voxire.pages.dev/blog/best-pos-systems-lebanese-restaurants-2026/)). Behind them sits a layer of ERP houses for which POS is one module, including **IDS Libra** (about 500 client companies), **MAPOS** (with an offline till), **Nexel**, **SBS** and **Wizard** ([Libra ERP](https://libra.ids.com.lb/); [MAPOS](https://www.mapos.tech/en); [Nexel](https://nexelitsolutions.com/); [SBS](https://sbslebanon.com/)), plus hardware-led bundles such as **POPs LEB** (POSBANK) with dual LBP/USD pricing and Arabic/English receipts ([POPs](https://popsleb.com/products/pops-retail)) and a cluster of Odoo partners (Azkatech, NavyBits, Plennix and others) selling Odoo POS with third-party dual-currency add-ons that cost about $50–90 one-off ([Odoo apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/pos_dual_currency); [Azkatech](https://www.odoo.com/partners/azkatech-1740990)).

International products barely reach Lebanon. **Foodics**, the strongest Gulf restaurant POS, clearly has Lebanese customers: in 2025 it raised its transaction cap "only for Lebanon" because at about 90,000 lira per dollar a routine $115 order exceeded the old limit ([Foodics changelog](https://changelog.foodics.com/enhanced-support-for-high-value-orders-in-the-lebanese-market-3vkY0w)). Yet it lists no Lebanon office ([Workable](https://jobs.workable.com/companies/dNDv7kYXzuuC6vh18jDDQf.md)) and costs roughly $95–115 per branch per month in the Gulf, with no free plan ([Foodics pricing](https://www.foodics.com/pricing); [Foodics Kuwait](https://www.foodics.com/foodics-pricing-kuw/); [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/foodics/pricing)). Square and Toast appear only in generic directory lists ([SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/point-of-sale-pos-software/lebanon)); they depend on their own card processing in a short list of countries and are almost certainly not usable here, though that should be confirmed. **Loyverse**, a free app with paid add-ons that works offline, is the likely free option for micro cafés and shops, but global reviewers complain that payment types cannot be corrected, open tickets can be deleted without an audit trail, and accounting is weak ([Capterra Loyverse](https://www.capterra.com/p/150632/Loyverse-POS/reviews); [Fit Small Business](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/loyverse-review/)).

| Competitor type | Examples | What they do well | Where they are weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legacy restaurant suites | BIM POS, Omega | Deep restaurant features, local support, reseller networks | Opaque pricing (BIM is listed both at "$29/month" and "$500 one-time per user"), technician-led installs, older review base (mostly 2015–2018) |

Three patterns matter for a newcomer. First, because CeevTech, MAPOS, Nexel, POPs, RTYLR and Odoo add-ons all claim USD/LBP, simply having two currencies wins nothing; doing it *correctly and quickly at the counter* might. Second, published prices are almost non-existent, and almost no local product is self-serve or phone-first, which is the most obvious open door. Third, most local strength is in restaurants (a rival's comparison even calls Omega "not best for retail" ([XStak](https://www.xstak.com/blog/best-pos-software-in-lebanon))), which leaves small retail comparatively underserved. The biggest caveat: no market-share data and no Lebanese user reviews were found, so the real switching pain of BIM or Omega customers is unknown and must be learned from interviews.

## The rules: USD prices, a posted rate, and VAT shown in lira at 89,500

The legal picture is simpler than founders often fear, but it has a few traps. Since March 2023 the Ministry of Economy has allowed shops to price in US dollars "exceptionally and temporarily", on three conditions: prices must be clear, the store must **post its exchange rate** (on shelves, at the entrance and at the till), and **the customer must keep the right to pay in lira or dollars at the register**, at a rate no higher than the market rate ([LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/economy/690020/minister-of-economy-issues-dollar-pricing-decision/en); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/mena/lebanon/2023/03/01/dollars-or-pounds-lebanons-supermarkets-begin-pricing-goods-in-dollars)). Inspectors raided supermarkets that failed to display the rate ([Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2023/03/lebanese-supermarkets-mark-prices-dollars-local-currency-tanks)). No later decision changing this was found, so the product should keep the pricing currency configurable per item, because some goods are still priced by the state in lira (a bread bundle was fixed at LBP 80,000 in September 2026 ([Libnanews](https://en.libnanews.com/the-price-of-bread-rises-to-80-000-pounds-in-lebanon/))). The exchange rate itself has been **stable at LBP 89,500 per dollar** since 2023, and since early 2024 the central bank's official rate and the market rate are the same number ([The National](https://thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2024/05/29/lebanon-dollar-price-cost-of-living); [Credit Libanais](https://economics.creditlibanais.com/Article/212041); [Middle East Insider](https://themiddleeastinsider.com/2026/04/28/lebanon-currency-collapse-april-2026-update/)). Stability is good news, but the software must still record which rate was used on every sale, because the rate has moved violently before.

VAT is **11%** on most goods, 0% on exports, with basic foods (bread, flour, meat, fish, milk, rice, sugar, oil, pasta, baby food) and raw produce exempt under Article 17 of the 2001 VAT Law ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/Lebanon/Corporate/Other-taxes); [VAT Law 2001](https://eduart0.tripod.com/sitebuildercontent/sitebuilderfiles/vatlaw_e02.pdf)). In February 2026 the Cabinet approved raising it to **12%**, but this needs a law from Parliament, and an IMF statement in September 2026 said it had not yet been enacted ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/02/17/anger-in-lebanon-as-government-raises-vat-and-petrol-prices/); [ANND/IMF](https://annd.org/en/publications/details/imf-lebanon-september-2026-reform-recovery1)). So the VAT rate must be a dated setting, never hard-coded. Businesses reportedly must register for VAT once turnover passes **LBP 5 billion (about $56,000)** over up to four quarters ([Staks](https://www.staksai.com/blog/accounting-software-lebanon)), which means many small shops are not registered and must not print VAT at all.

A valid tax invoice under Article 38 must show the seller's name, address and Ministry of Finance (VAT) number, a serial number and date, the customer's name and address, a description of goods, the amount, the VAT rate and the VAT amount; without these, buyers cannot reclaim VAT ([VAT Law 2001](https://eduart0.tripod.com/sitebuildercontent/sitebuilderfiles/vatlaw_e02.pdf); [BusinessNews](https://www.businessnews.com.lb/cms/Story/StoryDetails/4460/The-Ministry-of-Finance-gets-tough-on-VAT)). The Ministry has said consumer invoices must be issued **in lira including VAT** ([Lebanon Opportunities](https://www.everand.com/article/536761741/End-Consumers-To-Be-Invoiced-In-Lira)), and foreign-currency prices are converted for VAT at rates set jointly by the Finance Ministry and the central bank, which today means 89,500 ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/lebanon/corporate/other-taxes); [productivelevant](https://productivelevant.com/en/lebanon-vat-number-registration/)). In plain terms: a USD-priced receipt should also print the rate used and the lira totals and lira VAT. Books and supporting records must be kept for **10 years**, and statutory reporting is in lira ([Code of Commerce](http://data.infopro.com.lb/file/CodeofCommerce.pdf); [Azkatech](https://azka.tech/blog/lebanese-accounting/)), which argues for sales records that can be voided or credited but never deleted.

There is **no e-invoicing mandate, no fiscal-printer rule and no real-time reporting** in Lebanon today ([dddinvoices](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-lebanon)). That cuts both ways: incumbents have no compliance moat, but neither will a newcomer win on compliance alone yet. The Finance Ministry is preparing electronic invoicing and a receipt lottery with the National Lottery as part of its 2027 budget push, backed by a $41M World Bank IT project ([The Beiruter](https://www.thebeiruter.com/articledetails/2571); [Daily Beirut](https://dailybeirut.com/en/lebanon-news/technical-meetings-continue-between-ministry-of-finance-and-imf/); [World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099061026153065058/pdf/P181155-bf95a328-e262-4162-b3c6-55ee0d406f3c.pdf)). If that arrives, every shop on paper or pirated software will need to change at once, and a product already built with unique invoice IDs, a tamper-proof log, exports and a receipt QR code would be in the best position to catch that wave. Note that the tax findings come from summaries and secondary guides rather than official texts read in full; an accountant should confirm the invoice fields before launch.

## Lebanese shops run on cash, generators and the credit notebook

Cash still dominates. The World Bank put the cash economy at about **$9.9 billion, nearly half of GDP**, in 2022 ([The New Arab](https://newarab.com/news/lebanons-tanking-economy-cash-based-world-bank)), and the economy "still runs on dollars, largely outside the banking system" ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1551032/lebanons-economy-still-runs-on-dollars-largely-outside-the-banking-system.html)). Cards are growing fast from a low base: transactions rose about **130%** in the first eight months of 2026, and the card share at restaurants reportedly rose from 15% to 35% ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1547050/card-payments-surge-130-in-lebanon-bdl-outlines-measures-to-curb-cash-economy.html)). The central bank has capped card fees at 1.25% for supermarkets, mini-markets, bakeries, pharmacies and petrol stations and bans merchants from adding a card surcharge ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1505868/bdl-caps-merchant-fees-on-local-card-payments-at-125-in-various-sectors.html)), so the POS should not offer an automatic "card fee" line. Wallets such as **Whish, OMT Pay, WeePay and zaky** are paid by scanning a QR code printed by **Areeba** card terminals; Areeba runs over half of Lebanon's terminals and Whish claims "scan to pay" at more than 10,000 of them ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/areeba-rolls-out-qr-code-payments-in-lebanon/); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1339336/digital-wallets-a-new-way-to-bypass-banks-in-lebanon.html); [Whish](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/whish-money/id1284243483); [OMT](https://informative.omt.com.lb/en/news/pay-with-qr-on-omt-pay-app-and-turn-your-points-into-cash)). No public merchant API for Whish or OMT was found. The practical consequence: start by recording "Whish", "OMT" and "card" as payment types with a reference number, and later integrate once with Areeba (whose gateway has a sandbox and which mentions tap-on-phone) rather than chasing each wallet separately ([paymentproviders.io](https://paymentproviders.io/providers/areeba.md)).

At the counter, dollars and lira mix in the same sale. Customers carry $10 and $20 notes, lira serves as small change, and merchants typically give change in lira at a rate they set ([Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/lebanon-dollar-replaced-disappearing-lira-how); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1334524/should-you-pay-in-dollars-or-in-lebanese-lira.html)). A cashier therefore needs one screen that accepts, say, $20 plus LBP 500,000, and suggests change as whole dollars plus a rounded lira remainder; at night the owner needs two drawer counts, one per currency, that balance. This is the daily job the notebook and the free apps do worst.

Power and internet fail routinely. The state utility supplies about **4–6 hours of electricity a day**, 8–10 at best, and was still providing only a few hours in November 2026 ([L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1488062/following-a-fuel-delivery-delay-edl-to-reduce-its-output.html); [Energy Ministry](https://energyandwater.gov.lb/ar/details/101089/minister-joe-saddi-s-power-sector-reset-energy-minister-joe-saddi-lays-out-a-hard-line-reform-roadmap-to-rescue-lebanon-s-electricity-sector-from-debt-decay-and-dysfunction-1768810589); [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/11/03/lebanon-cuts-electricity-to-public-institutions-over-unpaid-bills)). Shops live on private generators costing around $300 a month or more ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/10/14/wake-up-and-smell-the-diesel-multibillion-dollar-generators-industry-fuelling-lebanons-corruption/)). Internet drops when Ogero's network or its diesel backup fails, taking Alfa mobile data with it ([NNA](https://nna-leb.gov.lb/en/economy/561763/alfa-internet-service-has-stopped-due-to-ogero-tel); [SMEX](https://smex.org/ogero-enacts-increases-in-fixed-internet-subscription-rates-in-lebanon-exploring-sustainable-solutions/)), and mobile data costs about $3/GB ([ts2.tech](https://ts2.tech/en/state-of-internet-access-in-lebanon-from-fiber-optics-to-starlink-skies/)). A POS must sell, print and close a shift with no connection, sync later in small compressed batches, and run on battery-backed tablets or phones that survive the generator switchover a desktop PC would not.

Customer credit is the human core of neighbourhood retail. Since 2019, local grocers have extended more and more credit to families who could not otherwise eat ([Asharq Al-Awsat / Reuters](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/2368861/falling-cliff-lebanons-poor-borrow-buy-bread)), with three-quarters of the population pushed into poverty ([PBS / AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/lebanon-leans-on-u-s-dollar-as-economy-tanks)). No source describes how tabs are tracked today, and no competitor advertises a credit feature, so this is a promising but unproven differentiator: customer accounts looked up by phone number, balances kept in the currency of the debt, partial payments, and a WhatsApp statement or reminder. It must be validated in the field before it becomes a headline.

Language and hardware close the picture. Lebanon is trilingual, with shop signs mixing Arabic, English and French and English gaining ground ([AUB ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.aub.edu.lb/items/6caca12d-5613-4283-9fda-d729d6a8fab4); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1414580/lebanons-tri-lingual-evolution-in-arabic-french-english.html)), and young people often type Arabic in Latin letters ("Arabizi") ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Lebanon)), so product search should find "shawarma", "chawarma" and "شاورما" alike. Cheap thermal printers sell locally from about **$18 (Xprinter Bluetooth) to $216 (Epson TM-T20III)** ([Ayoub Computers](https://ayoubcomputers.com/xprinter-xp-p501a-58mm-mobile-thermal-receipt-printer-usb-bluetooth-70mm-s/); [961Souq](https://www.961souq.com/products/epson-tm-t20iii-012a0-ethernet-pos-thermal-receipt-printer)); many cannot print Arabic text natively, so receipts should be rendered as an image before printing. For restaurants, **Toters** (Beirut-founded, 2,000+ partner stores) is the dominant delivery app ([Forbes Middle East](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-50-most-funded-startups/toters/)); whether it offers a POS integration was not confirmed.

## The prize is real but small, and 2026 is a bad year

There is no recent official count of Lebanese shops. The usable anchors are about **5,500 restaurants, cafés and nightlife venues in 2025** (down from about 8,500 in 2019), with **402 new brands registered** between early and late summer 2025 ([LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/press/693830/lebanons-restaurant-institutions-have-decreased-in-number-since-2019-r/en); [The Beiruter](https://www.thebeiruter.com/article/surviving-the-storm-inside-lebanon%E2%80%99s-resilient-restaurant-scene/566)); about **3,200 licensed pharmacies** ([Human Resources for Health](https://human-resources-health.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12960-019-0386-0/tables/5)); and a grocery sector where only about 120 are true supermarkets and roughly **60% of grocery trade runs through traditional shops and mini-markets** ([Hospitality News](https://www.hospitalitynewsmag.com/the-online-supermarket-sector-not-so-super/); [Catalonia Trade Portal](https://tradeportal.accio.gencat.cat/ca/mercats-potencials/liban/distribucio)). Adding the uncounted long tail of mini-markets and specialty shops gives a rough paying-POS market of perhaps 15,000–30,000 outlets, an estimate that needs validation.

Ability to pay is the harder question. Lebanon had a **69% software piracy rate** in 2017, among the highest in the Arab world ([Byblos Bank](https://byblosbank.com/common/economic-research-new/lebanon-this-week/lebanon-this-week-540/lebanons-piracy-rate-at-69-in-2017-37th-highest-globally-and-sixth-highest-among-arab-countries)), dealers sell POS bundles "with free software" from $199 ([OLX](https://www.olx.com.lb/q-new-po/)), and inflation is forecast at 17.5% in 2026 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/22/world-bank-projects-war-hit-lebanons-economy-to-contract-by-6-4-percent)). So the true competitor for most small shops is not BIM or Foodics but a notebook, a cracked copy, or a free app. The economy is also stop-start: 2025 brought GDP growth of about 4.2% and a 44.6% jump in tourist arrivals, then the March 2026 escalation turned the World Bank forecast to **–6.4% for 2026** ([BLOMInvest](https://blog.blominvestbank.com/tourism-activity-rose-by-44-59-yoy-in-2025/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/22/world-bank-projects-war-hit-lebanons-economy-to-contract-by-6-4-percent)).

A simple illustration shows the scale: 2,000 paying outlets with 1.5 tills each at $20 a month gives about $60,000 a month, or roughly **$720,000 a year**. That can sustain a small, lean Lebanese team, but not a venture-scale company. The regional precedent points outward: Wafeq, an accounting SaaS founded by a Lebanese entrepreneur, gets 90% of its usage from Saudi Arabia ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2024/12/wamda-capital-participates-wafeq-7-5-million-series-a-round)), and BIM POS grew by exporting through agents to the Gulf and Lebanese-diaspora markets in West Africa ([BIM POS Ghana](https://bimpos.com/gh/blog/the-dubai-expo-2020-as-the-largest-exhibitions-that-gathers-the-world)). Lebanon is an excellent place to build and harden a product, because if it survives outages, two currencies and cash-heavy customers here it will survive almost anywhere, but growth beyond a few hundred thousand dollars a year will come from abroad.

## What to build, how to price it, and how to sell it

**Differentiate on four things competitors do not do together.** The first is *honest self-serve*: a public price list, a free tier, and setup in under an hour on a phone or tablet the owner already has, with no technician visit. The second is *money that balances*: mixed USD-plus-lira payment, change suggested in both currencies, a posted and timestamped store rate printed on the receipt (with a warning if it is set above the reference rate), and two-currency shift closing. The third is *dayn done right*: customer tabs by phone number, in the currency of the debt, with WhatsApp statements. The fourth is *theft and audit control*: every void, discount and refund logged with who did it and when, which directly attacks a known Loyverse weakness and matters to owners who cannot watch the till all day. Offline-first operation underpins all four and should be marketed bluntly ("sells through power cuts").

**Must-have features before charging anyone.** Offline selling, printing and shift close with later sync; dual-currency pricing per item with USD as the default; mixed tender (USD cash, LBP cash, card, Whish/OMT as manual types with a reference number); a tax setting per item (11%, 0%, exempt) on a dated rate table ready for 12%; an Article 38 invoice template showing the VAT number, a gap-free serial number, the rate used and lira totals and VAT; a "not VAT-registered" mode that prints no VAT; Arabic and English with right-to-left layout and French as an option later; Arabic printing on cheap 58mm and 80mm ESC/POS printers; a never-delete sales log kept for 10 years with export; and an owner summary on the phone or by WhatsApp at closing time.

**Pricing.** The evidence supports a USD price list well below the Gulf: Foodics charges roughly $95–115 per branch, local list prices start around $29–40 per till, and Odoo starts around $25–31 per user ([Capterra Odoo](https://www.capterra.com/compare/135618-150632/Odoo-vs-Loyverse-POS); [Capterra BIM POS](https://www.capterra.com/p/143097/BIM-POS/); [Voxire](https://voxire.pages.dev/blog/pos-systems-lebanon-retail-restaurants-2026/)). A sensible starting structure, to be tested in interviews:

| Plan | Price (USD) | What it includes | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | One till, basic selling, USD/LBP, local data | Beat piracy, notebooks and Loyverse |
| Shop / Café | about $15–20 per till per month, or about $150–180 per year prepaid | Cloud backup and sync, dayn, two-currency shift close, WhatsApp reports, VAT invoices | Main revenue plan for retail and counter-service cafés |
| Restaurant | about $25–35 per till per month | Tables, kitchen tickets or screen, service charge, multi-branch, delivery-order intake | Full-service restaurants |
| One-time option | about $250–400 plus about 20% per year for support and cloud | Same as Shop/Café | For owners who distrust subscriptions after the bank collapse |

Collect in fresh dollars by whatever route the owner prefers: cash through a reseller, a Whish or OMT transfer, or a fresh-dollar card, with a discount for paying yearly in advance to cut collection effort. The one-time option is worth testing because, after the banking collapse, many owners distrust anything that depends on monthly payments or a distant cloud ([trade.gov](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/lebanon-trade-financing)); the honest pitch for the subscription is what a cracked copy cannot give: off-site backup against fire, blast or a dead hard drive, the owner's remote view, and support.

**Go-to-market.** Lebanese POS is sold through people, not ads. BIM POS runs a franchise model in which local agents keep an office, sell hardware and provide 24/7 support ([BIM POS franchise](https://bimpos.com/bim-pos-franchise-partner)), and Foodics uses no-upfront financing as a sales lever ([Foodics flex](https://foodics.com/flex-program-lp)). A newcomer should combine a self-serve route (free download, WhatsApp support and demos in Arabic) with a small network of hardware and IT resellers in each region (Beirut, Metn, Kesrouan/Jbeil, North, South, Bekaa) who install, train and collect cash in return for a recurring 20–30% share. For cafés and restaurants, the best moment to win a customer is at opening, so target the restaurant syndicate's new-brand pipeline and take a stand at **HORECA Lebanon**, which drew about 350 exhibitors and 24,000 trade visitors in 2025 ([Hospitality News](https://www.hospitalitynewsmag.com/?p=47728)). For retail, offer a "printer + free app" starter bundle using locally sold Xprinter or Epson printers, and test accountants and bookkeepers as referral partners (no Lebanese evidence on this channel was found). Sell lightly in 2026 and plan sales capacity around ceasefires, summer and diaspora seasons.

## How the phase plan should change

The proposed sequence (0 foundation, 1 selling MVP, 2 customers and cash control, 3 stock and reports, 4 restaurant mode, 5 growth) has the right shape, and the chosen stack (offline-first PWA, USD+LBP, Arabic/English RTL, VAT invoices, credit) matches what the market needs. Five changes would make it fit Lebanon better.

**Put the compliance and audit skeleton into Phase 0.** Several things are expensive to bolt on later: a sales log where nothing is ever deleted (only voided or credited), gap-free invoice numbers that still work when several tills sell offline (for example, a number range per device), the exchange rate stored on every sale with who set it and when, a dated VAT rate table instead of a fixed 11%, and a tax category per item. Phase 0 is also where to prove the riskiest technical bets with real hardware: that the PWA can print Arabic receipts on cheap Bluetooth and USB printers from Android, and that offline data in the browser survives restarts, low storage and days without sync. These are general web-platform risks rather than findings from the research, but if either fails, the whole product promise ("works through power cuts, prints on a $18 printer") fails with it, so they deserve to be settled before any selling features are built.

**Move two-currency cash control into Phase 1.** In a cash-heavy, two-currency shop, opening and closing a shift, counting a USD drawer and a lira drawer, and logging voids and discounts are not "phase 2 extras"; they are why an owner buys a POS at all. A Phase 1 that sells but cannot balance the drawer at night is not sellable in Lebanon.

**Bring basic customer credit into Phase 1 or the very start of Phase 2, and validate it first.** Dayn is the most distinctive retail feature available, but no source shows how widely it is used or how it is tracked. Interview 20–30 grocers and mini-market owners before building, then ship a simple version (customer by phone number, balance in the debt's currency, payments, WhatsApp statement) as part of the first paid release. Loyalty programmes and richer customer features can stay in Phase 2.

**Narrow Phase 4 and split it.** Full-service restaurants are the most crowded segment (BIM, Omega, CeevTech, RTYLR, Foodics), while small retail and counter-service cafés are less contested and closer to what Phases 1–3 already deliver. Add a light café mode to Phase 3 (item options such as size or sugar, order numbers, a kitchen ticket printer, and a service-charge line) so cafés, snack shops and bakeries can be sold alongside retail. Keep tables, split bills, course firing and kitchen screens for Phase 4, and only start it once the retail product is retaining paying customers.

**Make Phase 5 concrete, and add a pilot gate.** Growth should mean, in order: an Areeba integration (QR wallets and card or tap-on-phone) rather than separate Whish and OMT connections; delivery-order intake from WhatsApp and Toters if Toters exposes an API; readiness for Ministry e-invoicing (invoice export and a receipt QR code) as soon as rules are published; and preparation for the first export markets, the Lebanese diaspora in West Africa and Iraq, and then the Gulf, where Saudi e-invoicing rules make compliance features a buying reason. Between Phase 1 and Phase 2, add an explicit pilot: 10–20 real shops using the product daily for a month, through at least one outage week, before investing in stock and reports.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what features do we need?" to "whom do we beat, and at what moment?" For most Lebanese shops the rival is not another vendor but a free or pirated tool and a paper notebook, so the product wins only when it saves the owner real money or worry every single day: a drawer that balances in two currencies, a tab list that collects debts, a till that cannot be quietly emptied, and a backup that survives disaster. That argues for building trust and cash accuracy before breadth, and for treating restaurants, payments integration and regulation as later waves rather than the opening move.

Two outside events could change the game, and the product should be ready for both. A Ministry of Finance e-invoicing or receipt-lottery rule would force thousands of informal shops onto proper software within months, rewarding whoever already prints compliant, traceable invoices. And because Lebanon alone caps revenue at a modest level, the same hardened, offline, multi-currency core is the asset that makes expansion to diaspora and Gulf markets possible. The biggest unknowns, how common dayn tracking is, what incumbents' customers actually pay and dislike, and whether owners prefer subscriptions or one-time licences, can only be answered by talking to shop owners, and that fieldwork is the cheapest and most valuable next step.
